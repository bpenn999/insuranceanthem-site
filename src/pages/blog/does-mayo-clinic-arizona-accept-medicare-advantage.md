**For most plans, no. Mayo Clinic accepts Original Medicare, Part D and every Medicare Supplement at its Arizona campuses, but it does not accept Medicare Advantage plans it has no contract with, and the individual Advantage plans sold in Maricopa County during open enrollment are generally not contracted. A PPO does not get around that. If being seen at Mayo matters to you, the decision to make is between now and December 7, 2026, and in Arizona the order you do it in matters.**

I hear this question more in north Peoria than anywhere else I work. Households in Vistancia, Trilogy at Vistancia and Blackstone often moved here with Mayo on the list of reasons, and the hospital in northeast Phoenix is a drive of roughly 40 minutes east along Happy Valley Road and the 101. Then a $0 Advantage plan arrives in the mail, it looks like a good deal next to a supplement premium, and nobody mentions Mayo until a specialist suggests a referral there. That is the conversation this post is meant to happen before.

## What Mayo Clinic accepts in Arizona, by type of coverage

Mayo publishes its Medicare policy on its billing pages and repeats it to patients each year. In its own words, as reported when it went out of network with some Advantage plans for 2026, Mayo "participates in Traditional Medicare (Parts A, B, and D), and Medicare supplement plans" and is "out of network for most Medicare Advantage plans." Its insurance FAQ is blunter: it does not accept noncontracted Medicare Advantage plans.

Mayo signs Advantage contracts selectively and has been narrowing them, not widening them. For 2026 it went out of network with UnitedHealthcare's individual Advantage plans at its Minnesota, Wisconsin and Iowa locations, while the group retiree versions kept access. Arizona was already the stricter campus. If a mailer or a plan directory tells you an individual 2027 Maricopa plan includes Mayo Clinic Arizona, confirm it with Mayo before you enroll on the strength of it.

## Why a PPO's out-of-network benefit does not get you in

This is the misunderstanding that costs people the most. A Medicare Advantage PPO does pay toward care outside its network, and Medicare.gov says so. But the benefit only works when the provider participates in Medicare, is willing to treat you, and agrees to bill your plan. All three have to be true.

Mayo answers the third one in advance. Because it does not accept non-contracted Advantage plans, a Humana or Aetna PPO member asking for a scheduled appointment in Phoenix is typically told Mayo cannot see them under that coverage. The plan never gets a claim to pay. Patients on Mayo's own discussion boards describe the same notice: switching to an Advantage plan ends scheduled care there.

So the useful question is never "does my PPO cover out-of-network care." It is "will Mayo Clinic Arizona schedule me under this exact plan in 2027." Only Mayo's insurance office can answer that.

## What an Advantage plan still covers at Mayo: the emergency

There is one place the network does not apply. Under 42 CFR §422.113, every Medicare Advantage plan has to cover emergency care at any hospital, in network or not, and cannot charge more for it than it would in network. If you are on an HMO and end up in the emergency department at Mayo Clinic Hospital, the visit and the treatment to stabilize you are covered.

What the rule does not do is turn Mayo into your hospital afterwards. Follow-up visits, a scheduled surgery, a second opinion or months of specialist care are not emergencies. Once you are stable, the plan can expect you to move to in-network care, and that is where people who assumed "they took me in the ER, so they take my plan" get the surprise.

## The group plan exception, and who has it

Group retiree coverage is the one place Mayo access survives inside Medicare Advantage. Mayo's statement for 2026 kept UnitedHealthcare's group retiree plans in network even where the individual plans left, and Arizona public retirees ask me most often about the arrangement attached to their retirement system's Medicare Advantage PPO.

If you have a group plan, two cautions. First, the arrangement belongs to that group contract, not to the carrier, so a neighbor with an individual plan from the same company does not have it. Second, leaving a group retiree plan to buy something on your own can be permanent; some retirement systems will not let you back. The [retiree insurance post](/blog/do-i-need-medicare-part-b-if-i-have-retiree-insurance/) covers that trap. Ask the administrator about Mayo, and about re-entry, before you change anything.

## The way in for everyone else: Original Medicare and a supplement

Original Medicare has no network. Any provider in the country that takes Medicare takes it, and Mayo does. A Medicare Supplement then pays its share of whatever Medicare approved, regardless of the company or the plan letter. That is why a Peoria couple on Plan G can book with a Mayo specialist the same way they book with one at Arrowhead.

The catch in Arizona is buying the supplement. Arizona has **no birthday rule and no year-round guaranteed issue**, so outside a federal guaranteed issue window an insurance company can ask health questions and decline you. The windows that matter for a Mayo decision:

## How to make the switch without getting stuck in between

If you are on an Advantage plan now and want Mayo in reach for 2027, the order is what protects you.

1. **Apply for the supplement first.** If you have no guaranteed issue right, you need an approval letter before anything else changes. The [Advantage-to-Medigap post](/blog/can-i-switch-from-medicare-advantage-to-medigap-in-arizona/) explains why.
2. **Pick a stand-alone Part D plan.** Original Medicare does not cover most outpatient prescriptions. Arizona's stand-alone drug plan list shrank for 2027 and has no $0-premium option, so price it; the [Arizona Part D post](/blog/medicare-part-d-plans-arizona-2027/) has the list.
3. **Enroll in the drug plan by December 7.** Joining a stand-alone Part D plan during open enrollment takes you out of the Advantage plan automatically for January 1.
4. **Line up the supplement to start January 1** so there is no gap.
5. **Call Mayo's insurance office in January** with your new Medicare card and supplement card, and ask how new-patient appointments are requested for the department you need.

If health questions might be a problem, do not drop the Advantage plan on hope. Staying put and using Mayo for emergencies only is a legitimate choice. Losing both the plan you know and the supplement you wanted is not.

## Is it worth it for a north Peoria household?

That depends on what you are buying. A supplement plus a drug plan costs more each month than a $0 Advantage plan, and the difference is real money. What it buys is a predictable bill and the freedom to use Mayo, Banner, HonorHealth or a specialist in another state on the same terms. For households who split the year with a summer home, or who already have a Mayo specialist, that is often the deciding point.

Two things do not change either way. Income-related premiums follow you: if [IRMAA](/blog/do-you-pay-irmaa-with-a-medicare-advantage-plan/) applies to your Part B and Part D today, it applies on Original Medicare too. And care at Arrowhead, Sun City and the West Valley hospitals is available on many Maricopa Advantage plans, so the question is specifically whether Mayo is a place you expect to need.
