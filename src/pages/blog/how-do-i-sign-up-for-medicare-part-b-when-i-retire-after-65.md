**If you are past 65 and leaving a job whose group plan let you delay Part B, you sign up through Social Security with two pieces of paper: the Part B application and form CMS-L564, which your employer signs to prove you were covered.** The timing is the part people get wrong. Retire December 31 with coverage through that day and you can file any time from October 1 through January 31 and still have Part B start January 1, 2027. File in February or later and coverage starts the month after you file, leaving a gap.

A good share of the Turning-65 calls I take from north Peoria are not from people who are turning 65 at all. They are 66, 67, 68, still working, and they read the rules correctly the first time: a large employer's plan, so Part B could wait. Now the retirement party is on the calendar, often for year-end, and the question has turned from "do I need it" to "how do I actually do it." This is that how-to.

## First, check that you were allowed to wait

Everything below assumes Part B was delayed legitimately: you, or a spouse whose plan covered you, had group health coverage **based on current employment** with an employer of **20 or more** employees. If that was the arrangement, Medicare paid second or not at all, and the penalty clock never started.

If the employer was smaller, or the coverage was COBRA or a retiree plan, the rules are different and you may already be late. Read [Do I need Medicare at 65 if I'm still working?](/blog/do-i-need-medicare-at-65-if-still-working/) before going further, and if COBRA is in the picture, [Can I keep COBRA instead of Medicare?](/blog/can-i-keep-cobra-instead-of-medicare-at-65/).

## The two forms, and who fills in which

**The Part B application.** If you already took premium-free Part A at 65, which most people did, Medicare.gov sends you to Social Security's online Part B application. If you never took Part A, you apply for both at once. Either way, write the month you want Part B to begin. The default is not always the month you want.

**Form CMS-L564, Request for Employment Information.** You fill in Section A. Your employer fills in Section B, confirming when the group coverage started and when it ends or ended. If your coverage came through a spouse's job, it is the spouse's employer who signs. Medicare.gov's instruction is to fax or mail the completed form to your local Social Security office.

Section B is the slow part. Large employers often route it through a benefits vendor or an HR center in another state. Ask for it the week you give notice, not the week you leave.

## The timing rule: when Part B can start

This rule comes straight from federal regulation, [42 CFR §406.24(e)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/406.24), and it splits the eight-month window into two very different halves.

- **File while you are still covered, or in the first full month after coverage ends,** and Part B can start on the first day of the month you file, or on the first of any of the next three months. You choose.
- **File in any later month of the eight,** and Part B starts the first of the month after you file. No choice, and a gap if the job coverage has already ended.

Here is what that means for the most common case I see: last day of work December 31, 2026, group coverage through December 31.

So if you are retiring at the end of this year, **your window to file for a seamless January 1 start opened on October 1 and runs through January 31.** I would file in November. It leaves time to chase the L564, it keeps your card from arriving after you need it, and it does not rely on a January filing being processed quickly.

Two details that move the dates. Some employer plans end coverage on your last working day rather than at the end of the month, so ask HR for the exact date in writing. And the eight months are counted from whichever ends first, the job or the coverage. A severance package that keeps you on the plan a few extra months does not stretch the window, because the job ended first.

## If your employer will not, or cannot, sign the L564

Companies get sold, HR departments close, and a former employer has no deadline of its own. Medicare.gov's instruction is to complete Section B of the L564 yourself as well as you can, leave it unsigned, and send proof of the coverage. Social Security's operating manual ([POMS HI 00805.295](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0600805295)) lists what it accepts, and says the list is not exhaustive:

- income tax returns that show health insurance premiums paid
- W-2s showing pre-tax medical contributions
- pay stubs showing health insurance premium deductions
- health insurance cards with a policy effective date
- explanations of benefits paid by the group plan
- statements or receipts showing premium payments

Before your last day, download a year-end pay stub and every W-2 from the years you delayed Part B. That takes five minutes now, while you still have access to the payroll site.

## The other clocks that start when you retire

Part B is the one people know about. Three others start at the same time.

**Prescription drugs.** You have two full months after the month your group coverage ends to join a Part D plan or a Medicare Advantage plan with drug coverage. And you cannot go more than 63 days without creditable drug coverage without a separate Part D penalty. Ask HR for the creditable-coverage notice for the years you delayed, and keep it. ([More on whether you need Part D.](/blog/do-i-need-medicare-part-d-if-i-dont-take-prescriptions/))

**Medigap.** Your six-month Medicare Supplement open enrollment starts the month your Part B starts, not the month you turned 65. For a January 1, 2027 start it runs through June 30, 2027. Arizona has **no birthday rule**, so this is the one time an insurer must sell you Plan G or Plan N without health questions. Those six months are why delaying Part B while you worked was worth something. Picking your start date carelessly is how you lose them. ([How the Arizona window works.](/blog/when-is-medicare-supplement-open-enrollment-arizona/))

**Your HSA, if you still have one.** When you sign up for Part A late, it can be backdated up to six months, and you cannot make HSA contributions in any month you have Medicare. Stop contributing six months before you file. ([The look-back explained.](/blog/can-i-contribute-to-an-hsa-while-on-medicare/))

## What it costs, and the bill that surprises people

The standard Part B premium is {{partB.premium}} a month in {{year}}, with a {{partB.deductible}} deductible. CMS has not published the 2027 figures yet. It usually does later in the fall, and a January starter pays the 2027 amount.

The surprise for most of the households I work with is IRMAA, the income-related surcharge. A Part B start in 2027 is priced from your **2025** tax return, which is a full year of salary, often with a bonus or deferred compensation on top. If your income drops when you retire, that is a "work stoppage" under Social Security's rules, and Form SSA-44 asks them to use your new, lower income instead. File it once your retirement date is fixed. Do not wait for the first bill. ([How the SSA-44 appeal works.](/blog/how-do-i-appeal-irmaa-after-retirement/)) For the tax side, especially anything to do with deferred comp or a Roth conversion in the retirement year, talk to your tax advisor.

## A checklist for a December 31, 2026 retirement

1. **This week:** ask HR, in writing, for the exact date your group coverage ends and for a completed CMS-L564. Ask for the Part D creditable-coverage notice in the same email.
2. **Before your last day:** download your W-2s and a recent pay stub showing premium deductions, in case the L564 never arrives.
3. **In November:** file for Part B (and Part A, if you never took it) through Social Security, ask for a **January 1, 2027** start, and fax or mail the L564.
4. **If you have an HSA:** confirm your contributions stopped six months back from the date you file.
5. **November or December:** choose your drug coverage, and either a Medicare Supplement or a Medicare Advantage plan, for January 1. Use 2027 prices and 2027 networks.
6. **Once you know your retirement date:** file Form SSA-44 if your income is about to fall.
7. **Mark August 31, 2027** as the absolute last day of your Part B window, in case anything goes wrong.
