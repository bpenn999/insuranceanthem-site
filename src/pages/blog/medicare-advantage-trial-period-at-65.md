**Yes, once. If you join a Medicare Advantage plan when you first become eligible for Medicare at 65, federal law gives you 12 months to change your mind: you can leave the plan for Original Medicare at any point in that first year, and a Medigap insurer has to sell you any policy it offers in Arizona with no health questions, as long as you apply no later than 63 days after the Advantage coverage ends.** It is called the trial right. It is the last no-questions door Arizona gives you, because the state has added nothing behind it.

I bring it up with almost every client turning 65 in the north end of Peoria who is leaning toward an Advantage plan. Not to steer them off it. A good share of them pick Advantage, and many stay happily. The point is that the decision at 65 does not have to be made blind, provided you know the clock exists and what it takes to use it.

## Who actually gets the trial right

There are two versions in the statute, and only one of them is about turning 65.

**The age-65 version.** Under 42 U.S.C. § 1395ss(s)(3)(B)(vi), you qualify if, "upon first becoming eligible for benefits under part A at age 65," you enroll in a Medicare Advantage plan (or a PACE program) and leave it "not later than 12 months after the effective date of such enrollment." The enrollment rule that lets you leave mid-year, 42 CFR § 422.62(c), is narrower still: it applies to an Advantage plan chosen in the initial enrollment period around your 65th birthday.

**The "dropped my Medigap" version.** Under (B)(v), someone who already had a Medigap policy, dropped it to try Advantage for the first time, and leaves within 12 months can go back. That right is weaker. You get the same policy back from the same company if it is still sold, and only a short list of plan letters if it is not. It is mostly for people already on Medicare, so I will leave it there.

Who does **not** get the age-65 version, in the cases I see most:

- **People who worked past 65 and joined Advantage later.** If you kept your employer plan at 65, took Part B at 67 and chose an Advantage plan then, the statute's "upon first becoming eligible for Part A at 65" wording is a poor fit. Your six-month Medigap open enrollment starts with that later Part B, which is the protection you can count on. Do not plan on a second year after it.
- **Anyone past month 12.** The right ends with the first year. Liking the plan for 11 months and then hitting a network problem in month 14 leaves you with an underwritten application.
- **Anyone who leaves without a Medigap plan approved.** The right covers the application. It does not hold a policy open for you while you think.

## The dates for a 2027 start, worked through

Two federal clocks run at once, and the trial right is the longer one.

Say you turn 65 in February 2027, sign up ahead of time, and your Part B and an Advantage plan both start February 1, 2027. Your six-month Medigap open enrollment period, which runs whether or not you use it, closes July 31, 2027. The trial right lets you leave the Advantage plan any time through January 31, 2028, the end of its first 12 months. For a voluntary move, § 1395ss(s)(3)(E)(iv) sets the Medigap application window from 60 days before the date your Advantage coverage ends to 63 days after it.

Two things the table makes plain. First, for this person the trial right more than doubles the no-questions period, from six months to roughly a year and two months. Second, for anyone whose Advantage plan starts between February and October, the fall enrollment period falls inside the trial year, so the natural time to decide is the October of your first year, when the [Annual Notice of Change](/blog/medicare-annual-notice-of-change-letter-2027/) shows you what the plan will cost next year.

## The order of operations, which is where it goes wrong

The three moves have to happen in the right order, and the dates have to meet.

1. **Get the Medigap policy approved first.** Apply inside the 60-day lead-in, say on the application that you are using the Medicare Advantage trial right, and ask for a start date that matches the first day of Original Medicare. Keep a copy of the Advantage plan's enrollment confirmation showing its effective date; that is your proof of eligibility.
2. **Pick a stand-alone Part D plan.** The Medigap plans sold to anyone new to Medicare do not cover prescriptions. 42 CFR § 423.38(c)(5) gives you a special enrollment period to join a drug plan when you leave Advantage under the age-65 rule, and joining a stand-alone drug plan is one way to end the Advantage plan, because you cannot be in both and Medicare moves you to Original Medicare. Let more than 63 days pass without creditable drug coverage and the Part D late penalty starts counting, and [it does not go away](/blog/do-i-need-medicare-part-d-if-i-dont-take-prescriptions/).
3. **Confirm the Advantage plan has ended** on the date you expect. Original Medicare and the Medigap policy should begin the same morning.

What I have seen go wrong is almost always step one coming last. Someone ends the Advantage plan in a frustrated phone call in March, assumes the Supplement is a formality, and applies in June. By then the 63 days are gone, and in Arizona the next application is a health application. [Can I switch from Medicare Advantage to Medigap in Arizona?](/blog/can-i-switch-from-medicare-advantage-to-medigap-in-arizona/) describes what that underwriting looks like.

## Why the trial year matters more in Arizona

Many states give Medigap buyers some later chance: a yearly birthday window, or year-round guaranteed issue. Arizona has neither. Outside open enrollment and the federal guaranteed issue situations, a Medigap insurer here can ask about your health and say no. So the trial right is not a technicality in Maricopa County. For many people it is the only second chance there is.

There is also a cost reason to take the test year seriously. The Advantage plan that looks cheapest at 65 is judged on premium, but its real exposure is the in-network out-of-pocket maximum. CMS raised the ceiling plans may set to $9,850 for 2027, and many Maricopa plans moved theirs up; I set out the figures in [the 2027 out-of-pocket maximum for Peoria](/blog/medicare-advantage-out-of-pocket-maximum-peoria-az-2027/). A Medigap Plan G, by contrast, leaves you with the {{year}} Part B deductible of {{partB.deductible}} and a higher monthly premium. Neither is the right answer for everyone. The trial year is how you find out which one fits your actual year of care, with your actual doctors, before the choice closes.

## How north Peoria households use the year

The trial is a test, so test the things that decide it. These are the ones I watch with clients in 85383 and along the Happy Valley corridor.

**The specialist drive.** Most of north Peoria's specialist care is a drive to Arrowhead or to Sun City, and the network question is whether *your* cardiologist, dermatologist and orthopedist there are in the plan, not whether the hospital is. Make the appointments you would make anyway in the first few months, and notice whether a referral, a prior authorization or an out-of-network letter gets in the way. [What happens if my doctor leaves my Medicare Advantage network?](/blog/what-if-my-doctor-leaves-my-medicare-advantage-network/) covers what to do when one does mid-year.

**The second home.** Vistancia and Trilogy have plenty of households that spend summer somewhere cooler. An Advantage plan's network is local; outside it you are usually covered for emergencies and urgent care, and little else. Spend one summer on the plan before the trial runs out, not after. A Medigap policy pays wherever Original Medicare is accepted, in Peoria in February and at the lake in July.

**The ZIP seam.** Plan line-ups change across the Peoria, Glendale and Sun City boundary, sometimes within a mile. If you are moving within the Valley during your first year, check the new ZIP before the move, as [Do Medicare plans change by ZIP code in Peoria?](/blog/medicare-plans-by-zip-code-peoria-az/) explains. The [Peoria service-area page](/service-area/peoria-az/) has the north–south picture.

**The recent arrival.** If you turned 65 in another state and joined an Advantage plan there, then moved to Peoria within the year, the trial right came with you; it is federal. The plan did not, since a move out of its area ends it. That move opens its own enrollment period, and the dates get tangled quickly. Lay them out before choosing a new Arizona plan.

## Questions to settle before you choose Advantage at 65

- **What is my Advantage plan's effective date?** Write the first anniversary on the calendar, and a reminder 60 days earlier.
- **Would I ever want a Supplement?** If the honest answer is "probably, once I am traveling more" or "if my health changes," the first year is when that is cheap to act on. After it, the answer belongs to an underwriter.
- **Am I really in my initial enrollment period?** If you are past 65 and coming off an employer plan, your protection is the six-month open enrollment, not the trial year. [Do I need to sign up for Medicare at 65 if I'm still working?](/blog/do-i-need-medicare-at-65-if-still-working/) sorts that out, and [When is open enrollment for Medicare Supplement plans in Arizona?](/blog/when-is-medicare-supplement-open-enrollment-arizona/) has the dates.
- **What would a Supplement and a drug plan cost me today?** Get a real quote for your ZIP before you start the Advantage plan, so the comparison in month ten is against a number, not a guess. [Medicare Advantage vs. Medigap](/learn/medicare-advantage-vs-medigap/) is the longer version of that fork.
