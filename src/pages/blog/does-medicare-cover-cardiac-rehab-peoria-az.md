**Yes. Medicare Part B covers cardiac rehab after a heart attack, bypass surgery, a stent or angioplasty, a valve repair or replacement, stable angina, a heart or heart-lung transplant, or qualifying heart failure. The standard benefit is up to 36 one-hour sessions, no more than two a day, over up to 36 weeks, and on Original Medicare you pay {{partB.coinsurance}} of the Medicare-approved amount after the {{partB.deductible}} Part B deductible.** In a hospital outpatient program you also pay the hospital a copayment. Your Medicare contractor can approve up to 36 more sessions, and intensive cardiac rehab, a separate benefit, allows up to 72.

Those are the national rules, set in federal regulation (42 CFR 410.49), and they are the same in Peoria as anywhere else. What varies is the bill, the drive and, on an Advantage plan, the paperwork before the first session. Most of the calls I get about cardiac rehab come from a spouse, a week or two after the hospital discharge, asking whether the program the cardiologist mentioned is going to cost anything.

## Who qualifies for cardiac rehab under Medicare

You need at least one of these, and a doctor's referral into a program:

- A heart attack within the last 12 months
- Coronary artery bypass surgery
- Stable angina (chest pain that follows a predictable pattern)
- A heart valve repair or replacement, including the catheter valve procedures many people now get instead of open surgery
- A coronary angioplasty or stent
- A heart or heart-lung transplant
- Stable chronic heart failure, which the regulation defines narrowly: an ejection fraction of 35% or less, symptoms at New York Heart Association class II to IV, and at least six weeks on optimal heart failure treatment first

The heart failure line is where people get turned away. Someone whose ejection fraction is 40%, or who was diagnosed three weeks ago, does not meet it yet. The 12-month window on a heart attack is the other one to watch. A heart attack in December 2025 with no rehab yet leaves the window open until December 2026, not indefinitely.

The program itself has to include exercise your doctor prescribes for each session day, risk-factor education and counseling, a psychosocial assessment, an outcomes assessment and a written treatment plan that a physician reviews and signs every 30 days. It must run in a doctor office or a hospital outpatient department, with a physician or a physician assistant, nurse practitioner or clinical nurse specialist immediately available. A gym class, however well run, is not cardiac rehab for Medicare purposes.

Intensive programs combine exercise with heavy emphasis on diet, stress and lifestyle change, and CMS only approves a program after it has shown in published research that it lowered measures such as cholesterol, blood pressure and body mass index. Most people end up in a regular program because that is what their hospital runs. If your cardiologist suggests an intensive one, ask which approved program it is. A program that is not on CMS's list is billed as regular rehab, with the regular limits.

In practice, the 36 sessions usually run two or three times a week for about three months. If you need more, the request goes through your doctor, who documents why. A second qualifying event, such as a stent placed after you finished the first course, can start a new one.

## What a session costs in Arizona

Medicare.gov puts it in two lines. In a doctor office, you pay {{partB.coinsurance}} of the Medicare-approved amount after the Part B deductible. In a hospital outpatient program, you pay that and the hospital also charges you a copayment.

To get a real number for Arizona, I pulled CMS's own claims data for 2024, the most recent year published. For rehab billed from doctor offices in Arizona under Original Medicare:

- **Standard cardiac rehab with heart monitoring (code 93798):** Medicare approved about $24.71 a session on average. Your {{partB.coinsurance}} is about $5, or roughly $178 across a full 36 sessions.
- **Intensive cardiac rehab with exercise (code G0422):** 21 practitioners billed 3,891 sessions for 165 Arizona patients, at an average approved amount of about $124.55. Your {{partB.coinsurance}} is about $25 a session, or roughly $1,790 if you used all 72.

Two cautions. These are office-billed sessions only. Most cardiac rehab in the Valley runs through hospital outpatient departments, which bill Medicare a facility payment that this dataset does not include, so a hospital program will usually cost you more per session than the office figures above. And the Part B deductible comes first: if the heart event happened late in the year, the deductible you already met covers you, but sessions that roll into January start the new year's deductible over.

Ask the program for its Medicare rate before the first session. Hospital programs are used to the question.

Cardiac rehab is one of the clearest cases where the per-visit price adds up. Three sessions a week for twelve weeks is 36 copays. On an Advantage plan with, say, a $20 or $30 copay per session, that is $720 to $1,080 in a single course, which is why the plan's out-of-pocket maximum matters here. According to CMS's 2027 plan landscape files, the in-network maximums on Maricopa County's general Advantage plans run from $2,000 to $8,900 next year, so the ceiling on a bad year varies by more than $6,000 depending on which plan you are in. If someone in the house has a heart condition, the cardiac rehab copay belongs on the comparison list next to the drug costs.

On Medigap the math is simpler. Medicare decides the session is covered, the supplement pays its share, and the network question does not come up.

## The north Peoria version: the drive

From Vistancia, Blackstone or the Happy Valley and Lake Pleasant Parkway corridor, most specialist care already means a drive south toward Arrowhead or Sun City, and cardiac rehab is no different. That matters more for rehab than for almost anything else, because it is not one appointment. It is two or three a week for three months. A 25-minute drive each way is about 30 hours in the car across a full course.

Three practical points I raise with Peoria clients:

- **On an HMO, the network decides where you go.** Ask the cardiologist's office which programs they refer to, then check each against your plan's directory. The closest program is not always the in-network one.
- **On Original Medicare or a PPO, you can pick by location.** If a closer hospital program takes Medicare, you can use it without a referral fight.
- **Summer matters.** Rehab is supervised indoor exercise, and for a lot of north Peoria retirees it is the only safe way to keep exercising between June and September. People who finish their 36 sessions in May and then try to keep the habit outdoors in July tend to stop. Ask the program about a maintenance option, which Medicare does not cover but many programs offer for a monthly fee.

## If your Advantage plan is ending December 31

Several Maricopa County Advantage plans end on December 31, and if you are partway through rehab on one of them, the benefit does not disappear. Every Advantage plan and Original Medicare cover cardiac rehab. What can change on January 1 is the network, the copay and the authorization.

- **Before December 7,** ask the rehab program whether it takes the plan you are considering for 2027. If you do nothing, you return to Original Medicare on January 1 and have a Special Enrollment Period through February 28 to choose something else.
- **Expect the new plan to want its own authorization** for the sessions left. Have the program request it in December, not on your first January visit.
- **Count the sessions.** The 36-session limit follows you, not the plan. Sessions paid under the old plan count.

## The short version

- Medicare covers cardiac rehab for seven heart conditions, with a doctor's referral, in a doctor office or hospital outpatient program.
- Standard rehab: 36 sessions, up to two a day, over 36 weeks, plus up to 36 more if approved. Intensive rehab: 72 sessions in an approved program.
- Original Medicare: {{partB.coinsurance}} after the {{partB.deductible}} deductible, plus a hospital copayment in a hospital program. Medigap Plan G covers the coinsurance after the deductible. Advantage plans charge their own per-session copay, usually in network and often with prior authorization.
- Ask for the Medicare rate and the network status before session one, not after the first statement.
