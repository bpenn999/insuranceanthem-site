**Maybe — and the only document that answers it for your plan is already in your house.** Worldwide emergency and urgent care coverage is not a Medicare benefit. It is an extra that each Medicare Advantage plan decides to offer, and a plan may shrink it or drop it on any January 1. The place a member finds out is the **Annual Notice of Change**, the letter every plan had to deliver by **September 30**, in one row of the table that compares this year with next.

For {{nextYear}}, there is reason to go find that row. Insurers have said publicly that their 2027 plans preserve benefits; Healthcare Dive's October review of the filings concluded the data tells a different story, with the biggest carriers "paring back the generosity" of extras while Humana cuts roughly 2,400 plans and UnitedHealthcare roughly 690. On the travel benefit specifically, one report — a September 29 memorandum published for American expats in Panama — says UnitedHealthcare member notices show worldwide emergency coverage being eliminated on certain plans. That is a single report that names no plan, no state and quotes no notice, and I have not been able to verify it independently, so treat it as a reason to check rather than a fact about your plan.

What follows is how to check, what the benefit does if you have it, and what to do if the row changed.

## First, what this benefit is and is not

Medicare requires every Advantage plan to cover emergency and urgent care anywhere **inside the United States**, in network or not. Outside the country, the plan only has to do what Original Medicare does, which is almost nothing — Medicare pays for foreign hospital care in three narrow situations involving an emergency that starts on the U.S. side of the border, transit through Canada to Alaska, or living closer to a foreign hospital than an American one.

Everything beyond that is a **supplemental benefit**. Plans add it the same way they add a dental allowance or a gym membership, pay for it out of the same pool, and are free to change it with the required notice. Many plans sold in Maricopa County include it. When they do, it typically looks like this:

The benefit is genuinely useful for a Rocky Point weekend or a two-week tour. It was never a complete answer for a serious event abroad, because the cap is annual and modest and the flight home is not in it. [How it compares with Medigap and with a travel medical policy](/blog/travel-insurance-for-medicare-beneficiaries-arizona-2027/) is its own article.

## How to find the row in your Annual Notice of Change

The ANOC has a standard shape. After the cover letter and the summary of important costs, there is a section comparing {{year}} and {{nextYear}} benefits side by side. Look for:

1. **The row.** It is usually labeled **"Worldwide emergency/urgent coverage"**, sometimes "Worldwide emergency and urgent care" or "Emergency care outside the United States." It may sit under emergency services rather than under the supplemental extras.
2. **The {{nextYear}} column.** Three outcomes are possible. The copay and limit are unchanged. The copay went up, the annual limit went down, or a day limit was added — a reduction. Or the {{nextYear}} column reads "not covered" — a removal.
3. **The Evidence of Coverage.** The ANOC summarizes; the EOC for {{nextYear}}, available from the plan now, has the exact wording, the exact limit and the claims procedure.

If your ANOC never arrived, call the plan — the number is on your card — and ask for it, along with the {{nextYear}} Evidence of Coverage. A missing ANOC is not permission to assume nothing changed. The broader [guide to reading the letter](/blog/medicare-annual-notice-of-change-letter-2027/) covers the drug formulary, the network and the out-of-pocket maximum, which for most households matter more than the travel row — but the travel row is the one nobody notices until they are standing in a clinic in Puerto Peñasco.

## If the benefit is unchanged

Then nothing about this article needs to change your plan choice. Write down the copay and the annual limit, understand that the limit is the ceiling for everything abroad in a year, and decide separately whether the trips you actually take need more than that. For many Anthem households a short Mexico trip is well inside it. For a cruise far from a U.S. port or a month overseas, the gap above the cap and the absence of evacuation are the same whether the plan kept the benefit or not.

## If the benefit was reduced or removed

You have three options, and they are not mutually exclusive.

### Option one: another Medicare Advantage plan that still carries it

Between **October 15 and December 7** you can move to any Advantage plan sold in your county for {{nextYear}}, effective January 1. If you are enrolled in an Advantage plan on January 1, the **Medicare Advantage open enrollment period from January 1 to March 31** allows one more change.

The order of operations matters. Worldwide emergency coverage is a tiebreaker, not a first filter. Check your doctors — including the specialists you drive to — and your prescriptions first, then the out-of-pocket maximum, and only then compare the travel rows between the plans that survived those checks. Trading your cardiologist for a travel copay is a bad trade.

### Option two: Original Medicare with a Medicare Supplement

Medigap **Plans C, D, F, G, M and N** carry a standardized foreign travel emergency benefit: after a **$250** annual deductible the plan pays **80 percent** of medically necessary emergency care abroad, if the emergency begins in the **first 60 days** of the trip, up to **$50,000** in a lifetime. It is set by federal rule, so it does not move every January the way an Advantage plan's extra can.

The Arizona caution is the one I repeat every fall: **Arizona has no birthday rule**, and outside your one-time Medigap open enrollment window or a guaranteed-issue right, a supplement carrier can ask health questions and decline you. Leaving an Advantage plan in the fall and then being turned down in November leaves you on Original Medicare with no cap on your 20 percent. [Get the supplement approved first](/blog/can-i-switch-from-medicare-advantage-to-medigap-in-arizona/), then drop the Advantage plan — never the other way around.

### Option three: keep the plan and insure the trips

If the plan fits you in every way that matters and only the travel row changed, the cleanest fix is often to leave the plan alone and buy **travel medical insurance** for the trips you take. A travel medical policy is written for exactly what the Advantage benefit never covered well — the amount above a cap, long trips, and medical evacuation — and it does not care what your Advantage plan did on January 1.

## If your plan is ending altogether

A plan that is not offered for {{nextYear}} takes every supplemental benefit with it on December 31 — worldwide emergency coverage, the dental allowance, the over-the-counter card, all of it. You will have received a **non-renewal notice**, a separate letter from the ANOC, and you need to choose something for January 1. Whatever you pick brings its own list of extras, so the travel row goes back on the comparison sheet along with everything else. ## The short version

- **Worldwide emergency coverage is an optional extra**, not a Medicare benefit, and a plan may reduce or remove it any January 1.
- **For 2027, insurers are trimming extras**, and one unverified report describes the travel benefit being removed on certain UnitedHealthcare plans. Whether yours changed is in your Annual Notice of Change, in the row usually labeled "Worldwide emergency/urgent coverage."
- **If it is gone**, you can switch to a plan that kept it (October 15 – December 7, or one change January 1 – March 31), move to a Medigap plan with the standardized foreign travel benefit after getting approved, or keep the plan and buy travel medical insurance for the trips you take.
- **Even when the benefit is intact**, it has a copay and an annual cap and does not include evacuation, so a traveler's real question is the same either way.
- **A plan that is ending** takes the benefit, and every other extra, with it on December 31.
