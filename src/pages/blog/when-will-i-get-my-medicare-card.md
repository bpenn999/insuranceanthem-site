**Medicare mails your card in a welcome package about two weeks after you sign up through Social Security. If you were enrolled automatically because you already draw Social Security, the package goes out about three months before your coverage starts.** Either way, you do not have to wait for the plastic: once your enrollment is on file, a Medicare.gov account shows your Medicare number and prints an official copy of the card.

I get this question most from people in north Peoria who filed online, watched the confirmation screen disappear, and then heard nothing for a while. That silence is normal. The card is the last piece of the process to show up, and almost nothing you need to do in the meantime depends on holding it.

The phrase to notice in the first row is "after you sign up". Medicare's two weeks start when Social Security has finished with your application, not the moment you click submit. If you were drawing benefits already, my earlier post on [whether you get Medicare automatically at 65](/blog/do-you-automatically-get-medicare-when-you-turn-65/) covers the Part B box on that card in detail.

## Why "about two weeks" can stretch

Most straightforward applications from someone born in the United States, applying for Parts A and B in the months before turning 65, move quickly. The ones that take longer tend to fall into three groups:

- **Social Security needs to see a document.** A naturalized citizen or a permanent resident may be asked for proof of status, and a spouse claiming premium-free Part A on a partner's record may need a marriage certificate. Until that is in, the clock has not started. [What documents do I need to apply for Medicare?](/blog/what-documents-do-i-need-to-apply-for-medicare/) lists who gets asked for what.
- **You are adding Part B after working past 65.** That application travels with form CMS-L564, signed by the employer, to prove you had group coverage. A benefits office that sits on the form for three weeks adds three weeks.
- **The address is wrong.** More on that below, because it is the one I see most often in Peoria.

None of this changes your coverage start date. That is fixed by the month you sign up, under the schedule on Medicare.gov. A late card is an inconvenience. A late application is what actually moves your start date, which is why filing in the first three months of your window matters more than anything else on this page. The [enrollment timeline tool](/tools/enrollment-timeline/) shows the sign-up deadline that keeps your coverage on the first of your birthday month.

## Your Medicare number usually shows up before the card does

The card carries three things you need: your Medicare number (11 characters, and not your Social Security number), which parts you have, and the date each part starts. All three are available online before the envelope arrives.

1. **Create a secure Medicare.gov account.** Sign in through Login.gov, ID.me or CLEAR, the identity services Medicare now uses, and have Social Security's confirmation or letter beside you in case the sign-up asks for details from it.
2. **Choose the option to print your Medicare card.** It produces an official copy, the same one Medicare tells people to use when the original is lost.
3. **Save it as a PDF too.** If you split the year between Peoria and somewhere else, a copy on your phone saves a trip home.

If the account will not recognise you yet, the enrollment probably has not finished processing. Wait a few days, or check the application's status with Social Security, which handles every Medicare sign-up.

## Can I see a doctor before the card comes?

Yes, as long as your coverage start date has passed. The card proves you have Medicare; it is not what turns Medicare on. If January 1 is your start date and the envelope is still somewhere in the post on January 6, you have had Medicare for six days.

Give the office the printed copy from your account, or just the Medicare number. Original Medicare allows claims to be filed up to 12 months after the date of service, so a provider who sees you in the first week can bill once the number is in hand. If you are starting with the Part B deductible, it is {{partB.deductible}} in {{year}}, and the 2027 figure is due from CMS in mid-November.

The thing to avoid is the opposite mistake: assuming you have coverage because a card arrived, when the date printed on it is still in the future. Read the start dates on the card, not just the colours.

## Choosing a Medigap or Advantage plan while you wait

Every application for coverage on top of Medicare asks for the same details from your card: the Medicare number and the Part A and Part B start dates. Once you have those from your online account, you are not waiting on the post office for anything.

Two clocks matter here, and neither of them waits for the card either:

- **Your Medigap open enrollment period** starts on the first day of the month you are 65 and enrolled in Part B, and lasts six months. During it, any Medicare Supplement sold in Arizona has to accept you without health questions. Arizona has no birthday rule to give you another try later, so this is the window to use. Many insurers will take an application ahead of your Part B start once you can give them the number and dates; ask when you apply. [When is Medicare Supplement open enrollment in Arizona?](/blog/when-is-medicare-supplement-open-enrollment-arizona/) has the dates worked out.
- **Your seven-month Initial Enrollment Period** is also your window to choose a Medicare Advantage plan or a stand-alone Part D plan. Leave Part D too long without other creditable drug coverage and a late penalty follows you.

Expect more cards, not fewer. A Medicare Advantage plan sends its own member card, and you use that one at the doctor instead of the red, white and blue card. A Part D plan sends a pharmacy card. A Supplement usually sends one too. Keep the Medicare card safe even if you join an Advantage plan; you need it again if you ever switch back to Original Medicare.

## The mail-forwarding problem in north Peoria

Social Security mails the welcome package to whatever address it has on file. In most of the Valley that is fine. In the 85383 corridor, it is where cards go missing.

Two kinds of household run into it:

- **Recent arrivals.** Vistancia and the newer streets off Lake Pleasant Parkway are full of people who moved here in the past year or two and updated the DMV, the bank and the voter roll, but not Social Security, because they were not drawing benefits yet. The card goes to the old address in another state.
- **Part-year residents.** Trilogy at Vistancia and Westbrook Village both have plenty of people who spend summer somewhere cooler. A card mailed in July to a Peoria house with the mail held, or forwarded on a three-week lag, can take a month to reach them.

The fix is boring and quick: confirm your mailing address with Social Security before you file, and if you will be away when the package is likely to arrive, plan to print the online copy instead of waiting for the envelope. The [Peoria service-area page](/service-area/peoria-az/) has more on how the north and south ends of the city differ.

## What is not your card

The weeks around a 65th birthday are the busiest in anyone's mailbox and phone log. Most of it is marketing. Some of it is not legitimate at all.

Medicare does not call you out of the blue to ask for your Medicare number, your Social Security number or your bank details, and it never charges for a card. Calls about "activating" your new card, swapping it for a "plastic" or "chipped" version, or confirming your number before the card can be sent are scams. Hang up, and call 1-800-MEDICARE yourself if you want to be sure. Treat the Medicare number like a credit card number: give it to your doctor, your pharmacy and a plan you have decided to join, and no one else.

## If your card has not come: a short checklist

1. **Check the timing first.** Applied yourself: allow a few weeks from when Social Security finished processing. Enrolled automatically: the package should arrive about three months before your start date.
2. **Sign in to Medicare.gov** and print the official copy. If you can see your number and dates, your enrollment is done and only the mail is late.
3. **If there is no record**, contact Social Security. It processes Medicare applications and can confirm whether yours is pending, finished, or waiting on a document.
4. **Confirm your mailing address** while you have Social Security on the line, especially if you moved to Peoria recently or forward mail for part of the year.
5. **Need a replacement mailed?** Call 1-800-MEDICARE. Railroad Retirement beneficiaries contact the Board instead.
6. **Do not let the card hold up the plan decision.** The six-month Medigap window starts with your Part B date, whether or not the envelope has arrived.
