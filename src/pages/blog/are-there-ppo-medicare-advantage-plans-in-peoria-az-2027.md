It mostly does not. Roughly two-thirds of the general plans on the Maricopa list are HMOs or HMO-POS plans, and some of the brands people recognize from back home are HMO-only here.

Four things stand out once the list is in one place.

**Humana holds ten of the sixteen.** That includes the only two Maricopa PPOs on a 4.5-star contract for 2027, both under H7617. Aetna has three, Devoted two and HealthSpring one. If the plan you want is "a PPO from a different company than the one I have now," the choice is narrower than sixteen suggests.

**Twelve of the sixteen include drug coverage.** The four that do not, the Humana USAA Honor Giveback, the regional R7220-001, Aetna Eagle and Devoted's MA Only plan, are aimed at households that already get prescriptions somewhere else. More on why that matters below.

**Caps mostly rose.** Of the plans that continue, five carry a higher in-network cap for 2027 and none carries a lower one. The spread runs from $4,150 to $7,900.

**The Elite PPO is the only general PPO that ended in Maricopa County.** If that was yours, the [Aetna post](/blog/is-aetna-leaving-medicare-advantage-in-arizona-2027/) walks through what replaces it and when a Medicare Supplement is the better replacement.

## Which companies sell no PPO here

The plans missing from that table are the surprise for most new arrivals. For 2027, **UnitedHealthcare, AZ Blue, SCAN, Banner, Alignment, Wellcare, Wellpoint, Verda and eternalHealth offer no general PPO in Maricopa County.** Their Maricopa plans are HMOs or HMO-POS plans.

The UnitedHealthcare case causes the most confusion, because AARP-branded PPOs are common in the Midwest and people expect to find the same plan here. In Maricopa County the AARP plans are **HMO-POS**: a point-of-service option bolted onto an HMO. Medicare.gov describes it as an HMO that may let you get some services out of network, for a higher copay or coinsurance. Which services, and how much, are written into each plan's Evidence of Coverage. It is a real benefit, and for some households it is enough, but it is not the same thing as a PPO, and the HMO's referral and network rules still sit underneath it.

If you are not sure which kind of plan you are on now, the two or three letters after the plan name on your member card are the answer. [What each type means for seeing a specialist](/blog/do-i-need-a-referral-medicare-advantage-peoria-az/) is covered in the referral post.

## What a PPO costs that the brochure does not lead with

A PPO's appeal is freedom: no referral to see a specialist, no primary care doctor to choose, and the option to see providers outside the network. Medicare.gov confirms all three. Each comes with a cost worth knowing before you choose one for that reason.

**Out of network is a second price list, and a second cap.** Federal rules at 42 CFR §422.100(f) require every local PPO to set two out-of-pocket limits: the in-network cap in the table above, and a higher combined cap that counts in-network and out-of-network spending together. A household that expects to use out-of-network doctors regularly should budget against the combined figure, which is in the plan's Summary of Benefits, not against the headline number.

**The doctor still has to say yes.** Out-of-network care is covered when the provider participates in Medicare or accepts assignment, and is willing to see you and bill your plan. A Sun City or Scottsdale specialist outside the network can decline. Call the office and ask that specific question, not "do you take Humana," before you count on it.

**A PPO without drug coverage locks you out of Part D.** Medicare.gov is direct about this: join a PPO with no drug coverage and you cannot also join a stand-alone drug plan. Those four no-drug PPOs make sense for veterans filling prescriptions through the VA and for military retirees with [TRICARE for Life](/blog/do-i-need-medicare-part-b-if-i-have-tricare-for-life/). For anyone else, they can leave you with no drug coverage at all, and a gap in creditable drug coverage is how a lifelong Part D penalty begins.

**Prior authorization still applies.** No referral is not the same as no approval. A PPO can still require the plan to approve an MRI, a surgery or a specialty drug in advance, in network or out.

**Giveback is a separate decision.** Four of the Maricopa PPOs are Giveback plans that reduce your Part B premium of {{partB.premium}} a month. That is worth something, and it usually comes with a higher cap or thinner extras. [Whether the giveback is worth it](/blog/is-the-medicare-part-b-giveback-worth-it-peoria-az/) works through the trade-off with a Peoria example.

On the star ratings in the table: they measure a contract's scores on CMS quality and member-experience measures across all of its plans. They are useful for comparing companies, but they do not tell you how a particular Arrowhead cardiologist practice will treat you. [The 2027 Arizona ratings post](/blog/are-there-any-5-star-medicare-advantage-plans-in-arizona-2027/) has the full picture.

## Who in north Peoria a PPO tends to suit

From the conversations I have had in Peoria, a Maricopa PPO tends to fit three kinds of households:

- **The specialist-heavy household whose doctors are split across groups.** North Peoria care runs in several directions: Arrowhead for one specialist, Sun City for another, sometimes Scottsdale or downtown Phoenix for a third. An HMO built around one hospital system may reach two of the three. A PPO can cover the third out of network at a higher cost instead of not at all.
- **The household that does not want referrals.** Some people simply want to call the dermatologist directly. That is a legitimate reason, and it is what PPOs are built for.
- **The household gone a month or two a year.** A PPO's out-of-network benefit can travel better than an HMO's emergency-only coverage, within the limits above. [How far it travels](/blog/can-i-use-my-medicare-advantage-plan-in-another-state/) differs by plan.

And it tends to suit two kinds of household less well:

- **Households gone four or more months a year, or with doctors in two states.** At that point every out-of-network visit carries the higher cost, and a Medicare Supplement with a stand-alone drug plan, which goes to any doctor in the country who takes Medicare, often fits better. Outside a guaranteed-issue window, a supplement in Arizona generally means answering health questions, so that is a conversation to have before you need it.
- **Households whose doctors are all in one system.** If every doctor you see belongs to one group and that group is in an HMO's network, the PPO's flexibility may be something you pay for and never use. Check that network carefully, though: [networks change between years](/learn/medicare-advantage-networks/), and a "yes" for 2026 is not a "yes" for 2027.

The [plan-type finder](/tools/plan-type-finder/) asks the questions that sort those households, and takes about two minutes.

## The dates

- **October 15 – December 7, 2026:** the Annual Enrollment Period. Switch to any plan on your county's list, PPO or otherwise, for a January 1, 2027 start.
- **January 1 – March 31, 2027:** if you are in a Medicare Advantage plan on January 1, you get one switch to another Medicare Advantage plan, or back to Original Medicare, under 42 CFR §422.62.
- **If your 2026 plan is being discontinued:** your letter is dated October 2, and a special enrollment period runs through the end of February. [What to do with that letter in Peoria](/blog/medicare-advantage-plan-discontinued-what-to-do-peoria-az-2027/) has the calendar.
