**Arizona has nine stand-alone Medicare Part D plans for 2027, down from ten, and every one of them costs more than it does now. Premiums run from $5.30 to $191.50 a month. The four plans that charged $0 in {{year}} all charge something next year, HealthSpring Extra Rx ends, and the two AARP-branded plans from UnitedHealthcare roughly double.**

That matters most to the households who carry a stand-alone drug plan in the first place, and in north Peoria that is mostly the Medicare Supplement crowd. Vistancia, Trilogy at Vistancia and the 85383 corridor along Lake Pleasant Parkway hold a lot of retirees on Plan G or Plan N who pair it with a Part D plan, often one picked in another state years ago and never looked at again. If that is you, the Annual Notice of Change in last month's mail had the new premium in it. This post is the whole Arizona shelf in one table, so you can see where your plan landed against the others.

A few things the table says that the letters do not say side by side:

- **No plan went down.** The smallest increase is Humana Basic Rx at $6.30 a month. The largest is AARP Medicare Rx Preferred, up $94 a month, which is $1,128 more over the year per person, or $2,256 for a couple who both carry it.
- **The expensive plans moved the most.** AARP Rx Saver more than doubled, from $38.70 to $79.80. SilverScript Choice went up about 61 percent. AARP Preferred also more than doubled its deductible, from $130 to $300, and Humana Premier Rx, which had no deductible at all, now has one of $350.
- **The ratings and the prices do not line up.** The three Humana plans sit on a contract rated 4.5 for Part D in the ratings CMS published October 8. The two highest-priced plans sit on contracts rated 3 and 2.5. A star rating measures how the contract performed on CMS's drug-plan measures, such as member experience and pharmacy accuracy, not whether your drugs are on its list.
- **"Basic" and "Enhanced" are federal categories, not quality grades.** A basic plan is the standard benefit or its equal. An enhanced plan adds something, usually a lower deductible or better tiering on some drugs, and charges for it. With every plan now capped at $2,400 a year in out-of-pocket drug costs, the extra a household pays for "enhanced" deserves a harder look than it used to get.

## Why every Arizona plan went up at once

Two federal changes landed on the same year.

The first is that CMS ended the **Part D Premium Stabilization Demonstration** after {{year}}. It began in 2025, when the Inflation Reduction Act's redesign of the drug benefit was expected to push stand-alone premiums up sharply, and it paid plans to hold them down. AARP reported the program paid $9.8 billion in subsidies over 2025 and 2026. CMS announced in July that 2027 would go without it.

The second is that plans are now on the hook for more of the cost of an expensive year. The out-of-pocket cap rises from {{partD.cap}} in {{year}} to $2,400 in {{nextYear}}, and above that line the plan, the drug maker and Medicare pay, not you. A plan that pays more in its members' worst years charges more in premium in every year. That is the trade the cap made. [The 2027 cap explained](/blog/medicare-part-d-out-of-pocket-cap-2027/) walks through what counts toward it.

CMS's own projection was mild: an average stand-alone premium of $36 a month in 2027 against $35.09 this year, and a national base premium rising the full 6 percent the law allows, from $38.99 to $41.33. An average hides the spread. Arizona's cheapest plans went from nothing to between $5 and $10, which barely registers, while the plans that larger numbers of retirees have held for years took the large increases. Nationally, AARP's reporting of KFF's count has stand-alone plans falling from 360 to 312, and no $0 stand-alone plan anywhere for people who do not get Extra Help.

## The HealthSpring change, and the plan that ended

HealthSpring is the Medicare brand that used to be Cigna's and now belongs to Health Care Service Corporation. In Arizona its two drug plans go two different ways.

**HealthSpring Assurance Rx continues under the same name but a new contract number**, moving from a Cigna-era contract to an HCSC one, and its premium goes from $0 to $9.30. Whether a current member is carried across automatically or has to enroll again is the question your letter answers, so read which letter you got. An Annual Notice of Change means the plan continues for you. A non-renewal letter means it does not. Never assume from the name — the same thing happened on the Medicare Advantage side this year, which [the HealthSpring post](/blog/is-cigna-leaving-medicare-advantage-in-arizona-2027/) covers.

**HealthSpring Extra Rx ends.** It was the enhanced plan at $66.40 a month, and AARP's reporting names it among plans leaving multiple regions. If you are on it, your coverage under it stops December 31, 2026 and nobody moves you to a new stand-alone plan for you. Federal rules at 42 CFR §423.38 give you a special enrollment period because the plan is no longer offered. Do not lean on it: pick a replacement between October 15 and December 7 and it starts January 1 with no gap. A stretch without creditable drug coverage is how a [Part D late-enrollment penalty](/learn/late-enrollment-penalties/) begins, and that penalty lasts for life.

One difference from a discontinued Medicare Advantage plan: losing a stand-alone drug plan does **not** open a guaranteed-issue window for a Medicare Supplement. That right comes from losing the medical plan. If you already have a supplement, you keep it, and only the drug side changes.

## What a Peoria household on a supplement should check

The Medigap-plus-Part-D setup is common up here for good reasons — households who winter elsewhere, who see specialists in Sun City and Scottsdale and Arrowhead, and who would rather budget a known premium than manage a network. That setup puts the Part D premium squarely on your own budget, and for 2027 it is worth ten minutes.

**Run your own drugs, not the plan's reputation.** Put your actual prescriptions and dosages into the [Medicare Plan Finder](https://www.medicare.gov/plan-compare/) and sort by estimated total yearly cost, premium plus what you would pay at the counter. A $5.30 plan that puts your one brand-name drug on a high tier can cost more over the year than a $137.60 plan that does not. For a household on no prescriptions at all, [whether to carry Part D anyway](/blog/do-i-need-medicare-part-d-if-i-dont-take-prescriptions/) is its own question, and the short answer is usually yes, the cheapest plan, to avoid the penalty.

**Check the pharmacy you actually use.** Preferred-pharmacy pricing is where the stand-alone plans differ most within a ZIP. If you fill at a grocery pharmacy off Lake Pleasant Parkway and Happy Valley Road, or at a mail-order service, confirm that specific pharmacy is preferred on the plan you pick, not just "in network."

**If you are on one of the AARP plans, look first.** They took the largest increases on the shelf and a lot of supplement households hold one because it came bundled in the same mailing as their Medigap policy years ago. There is no rule that your drug plan and your supplement have to come from the same company.

**Remember the IRMAA line sits on top.** For higher-income households, the Part D income-related surcharge is added to whatever plan you choose and is paid to Medicare, not the plan. In {{year}} it starts at {{irmaa.t1.partD}} a month per person and reaches {{irmaa.t5.partD}} at the top bracket. Switching to a cheaper plan does nothing to it, and a forced plan change does not reset it either. The {{nextYear}} brackets have not been announced yet. The [IRMAA estimator](/tools/irmaa-estimator/) shows where your {{irmaaTaxYear}} return puts you this year.

**Know who is not affected the same way.** If your drug coverage comes inside a Medicare Advantage plan, these stand-alone premiums are not your premiums. CMS projected the Advantage-side drug premium falling for 2027. Your plan's own notice is the number that matters, and the [Peoria plan-by-ZIP post](/blog/medicare-plans-by-zip-code-peoria-az/) explains why the Maricopa Advantage menu looks the same from 85383 and 85345 while the networks do not.

## The short version

- **Nine stand-alone Part D plans in Arizona for 2027**, $5.30 to $191.50 a month, the same premium statewide.
- **No $0 plan.** Four were $0 in {{year}}; the lowest is now $5.30.
- **Every plan went up**, the AARP plans the most: Preferred $97.50 → $191.50, Saver $38.70 → $79.80.
- **HealthSpring Extra Rx ends**; HealthSpring Assurance Rx continues on a new contract at $9.30.
- **Change between October 15 and December 7** for a January 1 start, and price your own prescriptions, at your own pharmacy, before you choose.
