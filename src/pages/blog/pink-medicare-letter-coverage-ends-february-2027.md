**A pink notice from Medicare means Social Security cannot yet confirm, from its own records, that you are a U.S. citizen or hold an immigration status that still qualifies for Medicare.** If you do not confirm it, the notice says, "your Medicare coverage will end February 1, 2027." A federal law signed July 4, 2025 limited Medicare to U.S. citizens and nationals, lawful permanent residents (green card holders), Cuban and Haitian entrants, and people living here under a Compact of Free Association. If you are in one of those four groups, you keep Medicare. You just have to prove it.

This one is landing in north Peoria mailboxes in the same weeks as the plan-change letters, and it is easy to set it aside with them. Please don't. The people I hear from about it are mostly not who the law was aimed at. They are naturalized citizens who came from Canada, the U.K., India or the Philippines decades ago, and parents who moved here on a green card to be near grandchildren in Vistancia. Their Social Security file was never updated after they naturalized or renewed a card. For them it is a paperwork problem with a hard deadline.

## What the pink notice says

The notice is CMS Product No. 12238, printed on pink paper and dated December 2026. It does three things:

1. It lists the four groups that qualify for Medicare now.
2. It tells you to watch for a separate **letter from Social Security** that asks you to send documents proving U.S. citizenship or an eligible immigration status, "as soon as possible."
3. It tells you to send them online: sign in to your [my Social Security account](https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount/) and pick **Upload Documents**. The Social Security letter lists the other ways to send them.

People who get benefits through the Railroad Retirement Board get a version of the same notice that points to the RRB instead.

The order matters. In the final rule, CMS says Social Security expected to mail about 300,000 of its letters in November 2026, and the pink notice follows in December. Some households will see them in the opposite order, or only one of the two. Whichever arrives first, the deadline is the same.

## Most people who get one will keep Medicare

This is the number that matters most, and I have not seen it anywhere outside the rule itself. Medicare's Office of the Actuary projects that **about 32,000 people** will actually lose coverage starting in 2027. That is roughly 0.05% of Medicare enrollees. Social Security's estimate of letters is **about 300,000**. Put the two side by side and roughly nine letters in ten are going to people the actuaries expect to stay on Medicare once their record is fixed.

The Congressional Budget Office estimated a higher loss, about 100,000 people, and CMS acknowledged in the rule that nobody knows the real count yet. Even on CBO's figure, about two letters in three go to people who keep coverage. Either way, the letter is a request for proof, not a decision.

Two details that surprise people:

- **Social Security checks are not affected.** The law changed Medicare eligibility only. A lawfully present person who loses Medicare can keep drawing Social Security retirement or disability benefits.
- **The five-year rule for green card holders did not go away.** A lawful permanent resident who does not get premium-free Part A from work history, and has to buy Part A or Part B, still needs five years of continuous U.S. residence immediately before applying. A green card holder with enough work credits for premium-free Part A gets it without that wait.

## If you are a citizen or green card holder, here is what to send

The Social Security letter tells you exactly which documents it accepts, so follow the letter. For most people it will be one of these:

- a **U.S. passport**,
- a **Certificate of Naturalization** or **Certificate of Citizenship**, or
- your current **green card** (Permanent Resident Card).

Upload a clear copy through my Social Security, then save a screenshot of the confirmation. If you mail or take originals to an office, write down the date and the name of anyone you spoke with. And if the letter has your status wrong, for example it treats you as a parolee when you naturalized in 2009, say so in writing and include the proof. Do not wait for the formal notice that comes later.

On scams: this is exactly the sort of letter that copycat texts and calls will imitate. Answer it by signing in to your own Social Security account or using the contact details printed on the paper letter. Never use a number that arrived in a text or an unexpected call. Nobody from Medicare will call to ask for your naturalization number.

## What happens to your Medicare Advantage, Part D or Medigap plan

If Medicare ends, everything built on top of it ends too, and quietly. According to the final rule:

- CMS **disenrolls people automatically** from Medicare Advantage, Part D and cost plans once Social Security updates the record.
- The plans **are not required to send their own notice**. The Social Security letter may be the only warning.
- Social Security's formal termination notice was set for late December 2026, with coverage ending February 1, 2027 and appeal rights explained in the notice.

A Medicare Supplement (Medigap) policy pays only after Medicare pays, so it does nothing once Medicare stops. For a household that has just picked a 2027 plan during [open enrollment](/learn/medicare-enrollment-periods/), that means a January card in hand and no coverage in February. The cleanest fix is to send the proof now, before any plan change takes effect.

## Losing it and getting it back later

The final rule published November 9, 2026 changed one thing from the proposal, and it helps. If you lose Medicare because of your status and later become a citizen or green card holder, or you turn 65 before your status qualifies, you get a **Special Enrollment Period**:

- It opens the month you give Social Security proof that you now qualify, and runs **12 months**. The proposal said six. Older articles written on the proposal still say six.
- You can choose coverage that starts going forward, or up to **six months retroactive**, but not before the month you first met every requirement.
- There is **no late-enrollment penalty** for using it.

For the general rules on penalties and enrollment windows, the Learn shelf has [late enrollment penalties](/learn/late-enrollment-penalties/) and [special enrollment periods](/learn/special-enrollment-periods/) explained.

## Turning 65 in 2027 and not born in the U.S.?

The same rule applies at sign-up, and it took effect on July 4, 2025, so it already covers new enrollees. CMS is adding citizenship and immigration-status questions to the Medicare enrollment forms. If you were born abroad and turn 65 next year, put your passport, naturalization certificate or green card in the same folder as your Social Security number and employer coverage dates before your window opens. That way the application does not stall partway through your seven-month window. [What documents do I need to apply for Medicare?](/blog/what-documents-do-i-need-to-apply-for-medicare/) covers the rest of that folder, and the [enrollment timeline tool](/tools/enrollment-timeline/) gives you your exact dates.

## What to do this week

- Open every envelope from Social Security, the Railroad Retirement Board and Medicare before you sort the plan mail.
- If you got the letter and you are a citizen or green card holder, upload your proof through my Social Security this week and keep the confirmation.
- If your status is one of those that loses eligibility, get immigration advice before February 1. A change in status is what opens the 12-month window back in.
- If you already made a 2027 plan change, keep it, but make sure the eligibility proof is in first.
- If you help an older parent with their mail, ask them directly whether a pink notice has come.
