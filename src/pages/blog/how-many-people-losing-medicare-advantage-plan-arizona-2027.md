  - q: What happens if my Medicare Advantage plan is terminated?
    a: You keep Medicare. If you do not choose anything, you return to Original Medicare on January 1, 2027, without drug coverage if the plan included it. You can choose another plan through December 7 for a January 1 start, or use a Special Enrollment Period from December 8 through February 28, 2027.   - q: What happens if my Medicare Advantage plan is consolidated?
    a: You are moved into another plan from the same insurer automatically and stay in Medicare Advantage on January 1. You receive an Annual Notice of Change rather than a termination letter. KFF notes that people in consolidated plans who switch to Original Medicare do not get the federal guaranteed-issue Medigap rights that people in terminated plans get.
  - q: How do I know if my Medicare Advantage plan is being discontinued?
    a: Look at the letter your plan sent. A non-renewal notice, dated October 2, 2026, tells you the plan is ending and that you need to choose.     a: No. A terminated plan is a private contract ending, not your Medicare. Your Part A and Part B continue, and the plan keeps covering you through December 31, 2026.

**Nationally, KFF estimates 4.3 million people will have a Medicare Advantage or stand-alone drug plan terminated at the end of 2026: 3.1 million in Advantage drug plans and 1.2 million in drug plans. KFF did not publish state figures. Matching the CMS files for Arizona gives about 32,700 people: roughly 26,300 in Advantage plans and 6,400 in a drug plan. That works out to about 5% of Arizona's general Advantage drug-plan members, against 14% nationally.**

The Arizona count is my own arithmetic rather than a CMS or KFF statistic, so here is how it was done. I took each 2026 Arizona plan, county by county, from CMS's landscape file, checked whether it or a successor is offered in that county in 2027 using CMS's crosswalk, and added up CMS's September 2026 enrollment. KFF used June enrollment and a similar method, so treat the comparison as close rather than exact. CMS hides any county count under 11, so the real totals run slightly higher.

If you live in Peoria, the short version is that a low state percentage is no comfort if the plan on your card is one of the ones ending. CMS reports enrollment by county and not by ZIP, so there is no Peoria number, only a Maricopa one.

## The national names are not the Arizona names

KFF's national list starts with Humana at 530,000 people and UnitedHealth Group at 367,000. Arizona looks different. In the CMS files:

- **CVS Health (Aetna)** accounts for about 12,900 of Arizona's 24,938 general-plan members in ending plans.
- **Centene (Wellcare)** accounts for about 11,000. Most are in the Wellcare Simple plan H0351-053 outside the Valley.
- **UnitedHealthcare** accounts for about 730.
- **eternalHealth** accounts for about 280, and **Elevance (Wellpoint)** about 90.
- **Humana** has none in this count, because its Arizona plans continue or are replaced within the same county.

Those two parent companies make up about 96% of the Arizona total. That is a description of which contracts ended, not a verdict on any carrier, and each still sells plans here.

## The 13,500 people who are being moved, not dropped

KFF drew a line that most coverage skipped, and it changes what you can do. About 1.4 million people nationally are in plans being **consolidated**: merged into another plan from the same insurer. They are not counted as terminated, because they stay in Medicare Advantage unless they act.

In Arizona's general plans that is about 13,500 people, and two plans make up all of it:

- **HealthSpring Preferred H0354-001**: 13,208 statewide, 11,078 of them in Maricopa, moving into H0354-028.
- **Devoted Core 016**: 268 people in Pima County.

I need to correct my own earlier work on the first one. My [October 3 list of plans ending](/blog/medicare-advantage-plans-ending-arizona-2027/) compared plan numbers and counted H0354-001 among them. CMS's crosswalk is the better source, and it shows those members being carried across. In Maricopa that makes seven ended general plans, not eight.

The new plan keeps the name HealthSpring Preferred, so it would be easy to miss the change. According to CMS's 2027 landscape files, the plan these members land in has a $3,200 in-network out-of-pocket maximum and a $500 Part D deductible. Their 2026 plan had $2,750 and $200.

The bottom row is the one with money attached. Arizona has no Medigap birthday rule, so outside a guaranteed window an insurer can ask health questions and decline you. A healthy Vistancia retiree moved into a consolidated plan who wants Original Medicare and a supplement next year may be able to pass underwriting, and someone with a recent heart procedure may not. That is worth knowing before December 7, not after. My post on [switching from Advantage to Medigap in Arizona](/blog/can-i-switch-from-medicare-advantage-to-medigap-in-arizona/) goes through it, and the [step-by-step for a discontinued plan](/blog/medicare-advantage-plan-discontinued-what-to-do-peoria-az-2027/) covers what to do if yours is ending.

## Drug plans: a smaller Arizona number

KFF's 1.2 million figure is dominated by SilverScript Choice, which ends in 13 regions and accounts for about 950,000 people. It is still on Arizona's 2027 list, although its premium goes from $94.90 to $152.70 a month.

The Arizona plan ending is HealthSpring Extra Rx, with 6,398 members statewide in September. A separate 42,624 Arizonans are in HealthSpring Assurance Rx, which continues under a new contract number, S5715-045. Its premium goes from $0 to $9.30 and its deductible from $615 to $700, so it is a change to read about, not a termination. The [Arizona drug plan post](/blog/medicare-part-d-plans-arizona-2027/) has the whole list.

If you have a stand-alone plan that is ending and you do nothing, you can end up with no drug coverage in January, while Original Medicare carries on. Picking a new plan by December 7 avoids the gap.

## What to do this week

1. **Find the letter and the plan ID.** It is a letter and four digits, then a three-digit plan number. Check it against the tables above.
2. **Work out which group you are in.** Ending, moved or continuing. The letter's title tells you.
3. **If you are moving, compare the new numbers.** Look at the out-of-pocket maximum, the drug deductible and your doctors. Do not assume the same name means the same network.
4. **If you have a place in another county, check both.** Your plan has to serve the county where you legally live, so use that one.
