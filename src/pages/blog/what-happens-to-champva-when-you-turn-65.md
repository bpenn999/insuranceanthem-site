faqs:
  - q: Does CHAMPVA stop at 65?
    a: No. CHAMPVA continues after 65, but it becomes the second payer behind Medicare. To keep it, the VA requires you to be enrolled in Medicare Part A and Part B if you are entitled to premium-free Part A. The VA's CHAMPVA Guidebook tells beneficiaries to enroll about 90 days before their 65th birthday and then send a copy of the new Medicare card with VA Form 10-7959c so the CHAMPVA card is reissued without a gap.
  - q: Do I need Medicare Part B if I have CHAMPVA?
    a: Yes, in almost every case. Anyone who turned 65 on or after June 5, 2001 and is entitled to Part A must enroll in Part B and stay enrolled to keep CHAMPVA. The VA's guidance is blunt that if you cancel Part B, CHAMPVA eligibility ends on the same day Part B ends. The narrow exceptions are people who were already 65 before June 5, 2001 with Part A only, and people over 65 who were never eligible for premium-free Part A.
  - q: Does CHAMPVA pay the Medicare Part B premium?
    a: No. CHAMPVA is a cost-sharing program for care, not a premium-assistance program, so the Part B premium, including any income-related surcharge, is yours. In practice that premium is the main cost of keeping CHAMPVA after 65, because once Medicare pays first, CHAMPVA usually covers what is left on a Medicare-covered service and most people owe nothing on the claim.
  - q: Do I need a Medicare Supplement if I have CHAMPVA?
    a: Usually not for coverage. Behind Original Medicare, CHAMPVA generally pays the remaining balance up to its allowed amount, which is the job a Medigap policy does. The reason some households buy one anyway is insurance against losing CHAMPVA itself, for example through divorce, because losing CHAMPVA is not one of the situations that gives you a guaranteed right to buy a Medigap policy later. That is a decision to make during your six-month Medigap open enrollment, not after it.
  - q: Can I have a Medicare Advantage plan and CHAMPVA?
    a: Yes. The VA says a Medicare Advantage plan meets the Medicare requirement for CHAMPVA, as long as you still have Part A and Part B underneath it. The plan pays first and CHAMPVA pays second. The trade-off is that you take on the plan's network and prior-authorization rules, and leftover balances may need to be filed with CHAMPVA along with the plan's explanation of benefits rather than crossing over automatically.
  - q: Do I need Medicare Part D if I have CHAMPVA?
    a: No. Part D is not required to keep CHAMPVA, and the VA treats CHAMPVA pharmacy benefits as creditable coverage, so you can add Part D later without a late-enrollment penalty. The catch runs the other way. Meds by Mail, which ships maintenance drugs with no premium, deductible or copay, is only available if you have no other prescription coverage, including Part D.

**CHAMPVA does not end at 65. It moves to second place. Once you are entitled to Medicare, the VA keeps your CHAMPVA going only while you are enrolled in Part A and Part B, and from then on Medicare pays first and CHAMPVA pays what is left, usually up to 100% of its allowed amount.** Cancel Part B and CHAMPVA ends the same day. The Part B premium, {{partB.premium}} a month in {{year}} before any income surcharge, is yours to pay; CHAMPVA does not cover it.

The people this applies to are the spouses and survivors of veterans the VA has rated permanently and totally disabled from a service-connected condition, or who died from one. There are more of them in the north Valley than most people assume. In [Peoria](/service-area/peoria-az/), and especially along the Lake Pleasant Parkway and Happy Valley Road corridor in 85383, I meet couples where the veteran has used the VA for decades and the spouse has quietly carried CHAMPVA through every job change. Turning 65 is the first time the two programs have to be lined up on purpose. Get it right and almost everything that is marketed to a 64-year-old becomes optional. Get the timing wrong and there is a gap.

## The checklist for the 90 days before your 65th birthday

The VA's CHAMPVA Guidebook puts the instructions plainly: enroll in Medicare about 90 days before you turn 65, make sure Part B is included, and send CHAMPVA a copy of your Medicare card as soon as it arrives. In order:

1. **Three months before your birthday month, apply for Part A and Part B together** through Social Security, unless you are already drawing Social Security and are being enrolled automatically. Your seven-month window opens then; the [enrollment timeline tool](/tools/enrollment-timeline/) lays out the dates for your month.
2. **Do not decline Part B on the welcome package.** Automatically enrolled people get a card with Part B on it and an option to turn it down. For a CHAMPVA beneficiary that option is a trap.
3. **When the card arrives, mail or fax a copy to CHAMPVA with VA Form 10-7959c**, the Other Health Insurance certification. The Guidebook says CHAMPVA updates your record and issues a new CHAMPVA card with an extended expiration date. The card typically arrives a couple of weeks after you sign up, so leave room.
4. **Decide on prescriptions before your birthday**, not after: Part D, Meds by Mail, or retail through CHAMPVA (the section below covers how they differ).
5. **Decide on Medigap during its six-month window**, even if the decision is no (also below).
6. **If you have been getting care at a VA medical center through CHAMPVA, line up a new doctor.** The Guidebook says beneficiaries with Medicare cannot use a VA medical center under the CHAMPVA In-house Treatment Initiative. If you have been seen at the Phoenix VA that way, your 65th birthday is the end of it.

If you are 65 or older and not on Medicare at all, the VA asks for a notice of disallowance from Social Security showing you are not entitled, so it knows CHAMPVA is still your first payer.

## Still working at 65? CHAMPVA does not wait for you

This is where CHAMPVA households most often go wrong, because the general Medicare advice points the other way. Someone covered by a current employer's group plan can usually delay Part B without a penalty and sign up later in a Special Enrollment Period. Medicare allows that. CHAMPVA does not care. The Guidebook says that if you become eligible for Part A you must obtain and stay enrolled in Part B to be eligible for CHAMPVA, and that if you pick up Part B later, CHAMPVA eligibility starts on the effective date of your Part B.

So a working spouse turning 65 has two real choices:

- **Take Part B at 65.** At an employer with 20 or more employees, the group plan stays primary while you are actively working there, Medicare pays second, and CHAMPVA pays after both. That is three layers and you pay the Part B premium for the third.
- **Delay Part B and accept that CHAMPVA is off** until Part B starts. That can be reasonable when the employer plan is rich and the premium is high, especially with an income-related surcharge. It is a bet that nothing happens to the job or the employer plan before you re-enroll.

If you delay, the Part B Special Enrollment Period after the job ends is what protects you from Medicare's late penalty, not CHAMPVA. CHAMPVA is not employer coverage and does not count toward that window.

## What CHAMPVA pays once Medicare pays first

With Original Medicare, the claim does not need you. Medicare processes it and, per the Guidebook, electronically forwards it to CHAMPVA. In most cases the balance after Medicare, the 20% coinsurance and the deductibles, is paid by CHAMPVA up to its allowed amount, which the VA says is generally the same as Medicare's. CHAMPVA's own deductible is $50 a person or $100 a family each calendar year, and the VA caps a household's cost share at $3,000 a calendar year. Once Medicare is primary, most people never get near either.

The last row matters for the households I work with. Neither program pays for long-term custodial care, and CHAMPVA's Guidebook lists custodial care, housekeeping and attendant services as not covered. That is a separate conversation about [long-term care](/blog/is-long-term-care-insurance-worth-it-arizona/), and having CHAMPVA does not close it.

Finding a doctor is not usually the problem. CHAMPVA has no network, and the VA says any hospital that accepts Medicare, and its hospital-based providers, must also accept CHAMPVA. For Arrowhead, Banner Thunderbird or the Sun City and Sun City West hospitals, that is the question settled. For a stand-alone practice, ask whether it accepts Medicare assignment and CHAMPVA.

## Do you still need a Medigap policy?

For coverage, mostly no. CHAMPVA behind Original Medicare does the job a Medigap policy does, and you would be paying a monthly premium to stand in front of a payer that already picks up the balance. The Guidebook says that if you do carry one, CHAMPVA pays after both Medicare and the Medigap plan.

The case for buying one anyway is not about coverage today. It is about what happens if CHAMPVA goes away:

- **Divorce or annulment ends CHAMPVA** at midnight on the date it is final, according to the Guidebook.
- **Losing CHAMPVA is not on Medicare's list of guaranteed-issue situations.** Medicare gives you a guaranteed right to buy a Medigap policy without health questions when, for example, an employer plan that pays after Medicare ends, or a Medicare Advantage plan leaves your area. CHAMPVA ending is not one of them.
- **Arizona has no birthday rule or annual switch window for Medigap.** Outside your [six-month Medigap open enrollment](/blog/when-is-medicare-supplement-open-enrollment-arizona/), which starts the month your Part B starts at 65 or later, a company can ask health questions and decline you.

For a long, stable marriage to a veteran with a permanent rating, most couples I talk to decide the premium is not worth it. Where the marriage, the health of the beneficiary or the amount of money at stake makes the downside matter, a Medigap policy bought in the open-enrollment window is the only version of that insurance that does not depend on your health later. Whichever way you go, make it a decision during those six months, not a default.

## Medicare Advantage with CHAMPVA

The VA says a Medicare Advantage plan meets CHAMPVA's Medicare requirement, as long as Part A and Part B are still in force underneath it. The plan pays first and CHAMPVA pays second. What changes:

- **You take on a network and prior authorization.** If the plan denies an out-of-network visit, CHAMPVA's Guidebook says a cost to you is most often because the other insurance denied the service, and CHAMPVA then pays up to its own allowed amount under its normal cost share. That is a backstop, not a substitute for staying in network.
- **The paperwork is yours.** Original Medicare forwards claims to CHAMPVA. With other coverage, the Guidebook has you or the provider file the claim with that plan first and then send CHAMPVA the balance with the plan's explanation of benefits and the itemized bill.
- **Extras and the giveback.** Some households choose a plan for dental or hearing benefits CHAMPVA does not include, or for a [Part B giveback](/blog/is-the-medicare-part-b-giveback-worth-it-peoria-az/) that trims the one premium CHAMPVA leaves you paying. CMS's 2027 plan landscape files show several Maricopa County plans with a giveback. Check the network before the extras, and remember the plan's service area if you spend summers out of state.

You can also leave a Medicare Advantage plan you joined at 65 within its first year and go back to Original Medicare. The rules for that [first-year trial right](/blog/medicare-advantage-trial-period-at-65/) apply to CHAMPVA households the same way.

## Part D, Meds by Mail, or neither

Part D is not required for CHAMPVA. The Guidebook calls CHAMPVA pharmacy benefits creditable coverage, so if you skip Part D now and want it later you will not owe a late-enrollment penalty. That leaves three workable set-ups:

For a household on a few stable maintenance drugs, Meds by Mail is hard to beat, and the Guidebook notes it will ship to a temporary address such as a summer or winter home. Part D starts to earn its premium when you take a drug CHAMPVA restricts. The VA now covers GLP-1 drugs only for their FDA-approved uses and not for weight loss, for example, so the formulary is worth checking. Remember the creditable-coverage rule means you can change your mind at a later enrollment period without paying for having waited.

## Two other things that change at 65

- **Living abroad.** The Guidebook says a beneficiary 65 or older who lives overseas still has to carry Part B if eligible for Part A, even though Medicare generally does not pay outside the U.S. Overseas, CHAMPVA becomes the primary payer at its under-65 level of coverage. That is worth knowing if you spend winters [in Mexico](/blog/does-medicare-cover-me-in-mexico/).
- **TRICARE.** If the veteran is also a military retiree, or the household otherwise becomes TRICARE-eligible, CHAMPVA eligibility ends. That is a different set of rules, covered in the [TRICARE For Life post](/blog/do-i-need-medicare-part-b-if-i-have-tricare-for-life/). The veteran's own VA health care and Medicare are a separate question too, [answered here](/blog/do-i-need-medicare-if-i-have-va-benefits/).
