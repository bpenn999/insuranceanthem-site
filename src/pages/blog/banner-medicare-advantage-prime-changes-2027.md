**Banner Medicare Advantage Prime keeps its $0 monthly plan premium and its $2,995 in-network out-of-pocket maximum for 2027, but specialist visits go from a $15 copay to $35, the deductible on Tier 3 to 5 drugs rises from $440 to $540, and prescription hearing aids and routine chiropractic are no longer covered.** That is the summary of Banner's own 2027 Annual Notice of Change for the plan, which CMS lists as H5843-001. The plan is not being discontinued. Members who do nothing by December 7 stay on it with the 2027 benefits.

Banner Prime is a plan people in the northwest Valley choose for a reason. It is the HMO run by Banner Health itself, the system behind Banner Boswell in Sun City and Banner Del E. Webb in Sun City West, which is where a lot of north Peoria households already go for specialist care. In September 2026, 7,431 Maricopa County residents were enrolled in it, according to CMS's monthly enrollment file. If you are one of them, the headline numbers on your notice look reassuring, and the changes are further down the page.

## What carries over unchanged

The parts of the plan most people chose it for are still there:

- **$0 monthly plan premium.** You still pay your Part B premium, {{partB.premium}} a month in {{figYear}}, plus any IRMAA if your income is high enough.
- **$2,995 in-network cap** on covered Part A and Part B services, the same as this year.
- **$0 primary care visits**, $0 urgent care, $0 lab work and $0 outpatient X-rays.
- **Hospital stays at $275 a day for days 1 to 7**, then $0 through day 90.
- **No medical deductible.** Tier 1 and Tier 2 generics still skip the drug deductible: $0 and $5 for a month's supply, and Tier 3 preferred brands stay at $47 once the deductible is met.
- **Dental:** a combined preventive and comprehensive allowance of up to $1,000 a year, with comprehensive work at 50% and subject to prior authorization.
- **Worldwide emergency cover** with a $25,000 yearly allowance (the copay is the new $150).
- **Fitness and over-the-counter items:** Silver&Fit at $0 and a $65 OTC allowance each quarter.

Drug spending is capped separately under Part D, at {{partD.cap}} for {{figYear}}. Banner's notice confirms the plan follows that cap, so a household with expensive brand drugs is protected at the top whatever happens to the deductible at the bottom.

## Three north Peoria households, worked through

These are illustrations, not quotes. Your own numbers depend on your doctors, your drugs and how the year goes.

**The light user in Vistancia.** Two specialist visits a year, a few generics, no hearing aids, no chiropractor. The 2027 changes cost this person about $40 more in copays, and if a brand drug starts mid-year, up to $100 more in deductible. For someone in good health the plan barely changes.

**The cardiology patient off Happy Valley Road.** A cardiologist and an endocrinologist, about a dozen specialist visits between them, and a twelve-week round of physical therapy after a shoulder repair. Twelve specialist visits at $20 more each is $240. Twenty-four therapy sessions that used to be $0 are now $20 each, which is $480. Add the higher drug deductible and this household is looking at roughly $800 more for 2027, before any emergency visit. The $2,995 cap still limits the worst case. The real difference is in an ordinary year that never comes close to the cap.

**The couple who were due for hearing aids.** If you were planning to use the 2026 prescription hearing aid benefit next year, it will not be there. The plan still covers the annual hearing exam at $0, but the devices are now on you. Do this one before December 31: if an audiologist has already recommended aids, ask Banner's customer care line whether a fitting completed in December falls under the 2026 benefit. Our [hearing aids post](/blog/does-medicare-cover-hearing-aids-arizona/) explains what Original Medicare does and does not pay for.

The chiropractic change works the same way. The plan still pays for Medicare-covered spinal manipulation at a $20 copay with prior authorization, which is the narrow benefit described in our [chiropractic post](/blog/does-medicare-cover-chiropractic-care-peoria-az/). What ends is the routine, maintenance-style visit the plan used to add on top.

## Where Prime's $2,995 cap now ranks in Maricopa

This is the change that is easy to miss, because it is not a change to Banner at all. According to CMS's plan landscape files, 13 of the 46 general Medicare Advantage plans with drug coverage sold in Maricopa County in 2026 had an in-network cap of $2,995 or less. For 2027 it is 8 of 45. Other carriers raised their caps and Banner did not. That leaves Prime with one of the lower caps in the county. Our post on the [2027 out-of-pocket maximum in Peoria](/blog/medicare-advantage-out-of-pocket-maximum-peoria-az-2027/) has the whole county range.

For someone weighing a forced move off one of the plans that ended this year, that matters. A low cap protects you in a bad year. The copays decide what you spend in a normal one. For 2027 Banner moved some cost out of the first and into the second.

## The 3-star rating and what it covers

Contract H5843 is rated **3 stars overall** in CMS's 2027 Star Ratings, down from 3.5 in 2026. It scored 3 stars on the health plan summary and 4 on the drug plan summary. The rating covers the whole contract, it is built from measures such as screenings, complaints and call-centre performance, and it was set before the 2027 benefit changes. It is not a score for Banner's hospitals or doctors. There is more on how to read the 2027 ratings in our [5-star plans post](/blog/are-there-any-5-star-medicare-advantage-plans-in-arizona-2027/).

## What a Prime member can do before December 7

You have three real choices, and staying put is one of them.

1. **Stay.** If your doctors are in Banner's 2027 directory, your drugs are on its 2027 list and you are not a heavy specialist or therapy user, the plan you have may still be a good fit. Doing nothing keeps it.
2. **Switch to another Maricopa plan by December 7, 2026** for a January 1 start. Run your doctors first, by name and against the 2027 directory of the specific plan, then your drugs. Peoria sits on a ZIP seam with Glendale and Sun City, and plan line-ups and networks can differ by ZIP, so check the [Peoria](/service-area/peoria-az/), [Glendale](/service-area/glendale-az/) or [Sun City](/service-area/sun-city-az/) list that matches your address.
3. **Apply for a Medicare Supplement.** Possible at any time, but because Banner Prime is continuing, the change does not give you a guaranteed-issue right. Outside your first six months on Part B, an Arizona insurer can ask health questions and decline you, and Arizona has no birthday rule. The exception is anyone in their first year of Medicare Advantage after turning 65, who has a trial right to go back to Original Medicare and buy a supplement without underwriting.

Benefit changes like these do not open a Special Enrollment Period. If December 7 passes, your next chance is the Medicare Advantage open enrollment period, January 1 to March 31, 2027, which allows one switch to another Advantage plan or back to Original Medicare. Our [after-December-7 post](/blog/can-i-change-my-medicare-plan-after-december-7/) goes through the rules.

## The short version

- **Banner Medicare Advantage Prime continues in Maricopa County for 2027**, at a $0 plan premium with the same $2,995 in-network cap.
- **The costs that rise:** specialists $15 to $35, therapy $0 to $20, emergency room $120 to $150, and the Tier 3 to 5 drug deductible $440 to $540.
- **The benefits that end:** prescription hearing aids and fitting, routine chiropractic, the healthy food and utility benefits, and $150 of the eyewear allowance.
- **The network and drug list changed too.** Banner's notice does not name the changes, so check your own doctors and prescriptions in the 2027 documents.
- **The cap is now one of the lower ones in the county:** 8 of 45 Maricopa general plans are at or below $2,995 for 2027.
- **December 7 is the clean deadline**, with one more move January 1 to March 31.
