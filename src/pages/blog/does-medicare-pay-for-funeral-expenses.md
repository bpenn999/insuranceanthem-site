**No. Medicare does not pay for funeral expenses: not Original Medicare, not a Medicare Advantage plan, not a Medigap policy and not Part D.** Medicare pays for medical care while a person is alive, and coverage ends at death. The only federal cash aimed at a funeral is Social Security's one-time **$255 lump-sum death payment**, which goes to a surviving spouse who lived in the same household. Everything else a family spends on a funeral, burial or cremation comes from savings, the estate, life or final expense insurance, a prepaid funeral agreement, or veterans benefits.

The question usually reaches me sideways. A couple in Vistancia or along the Happy Valley corridor in north Peoria is reviewing their Medicare for {{nextYear}}, and one of them asks, almost as an afterthought, "and Medicare pays for the funeral, right?" It doesn't. Knowing that now, and knowing what does pay, is a much easier conversation than learning it in a funeral home office.

## Where Medicare's coverage stops

Medicare's last bills are usually the ones for end-of-life care, and those can be substantial. Here is how they are handled, and where the line falls.

- **Hospice.** Under Part A, Medicare covers hospice for someone whose doctor certifies a life expectancy of six months or less and who chooses comfort care rather than treatment aimed at a cure. That includes nursing visits, medications for pain and symptoms, equipment and support for the family. It does not cover room and board in a care facility, and it ends at death. If you are weighing it, the [hospice-at-home post](/blog/does-medicare-cover-hospice-care-at-home-peoria-az/) covers how the benefit works in Peoria.
- **Hospital and doctor bills.** Charges for care before death are processed normally. The patient's Medigap plan or Advantage plan pays its share, and anything left becomes a bill to the estate.
- **After death.** Nothing. Transport to a funeral home, embalming, cremation, a casket, a plot, an urn, a headstone, an obituary: none of it is a Medicare-covered service, and Medicare's [list of what it doesn't cover](/learn/what-medicare-does-not-pay-for/) is longer than most people expect.

A Medicare Advantage plan cannot fill this gap either. Federal rules at 42 CFR § 422.100 let Advantage plans add extra benefits only when they are primarily health related, which is why you see dental, vision, hearing and fitness benefits but not a burial allowance. A Medigap plan has nothing to pay once the medical claims stop.

## What Social Security actually pays

The $255 death payment is real, but it is narrow:

1. **It goes to a surviving spouse who was living in the same household.** If there is no such spouse, it can go to a child who is eligible for survivor benefits on the worker's record. Nobody else can claim it, including the friend or adult child who paid the funeral bill.
2. **It is $255, full stop.** The figure in the federal regulation has not changed in decades and is not adjusted for inflation.
3. **The larger survivor issue is the monthly benefit.** A surviving spouse may be able to move up to the larger of the two Social Security benefits. A household that was getting two checks usually ends up with one. That income drop matters more to a long-term budget than the $255 does.

What happens to the survivor's own Medicare after a death is its own topic: the [post on what happens to your Medicare when your spouse dies](/blog/what-happens-to-my-medicare-when-my-spouse-dies/) walks through plans, IRMAA and the paperwork.

## What does pay for a funeral in Arizona

For most Peoria and North Phoenix households, a funeral is paid by some combination of the following. They are listed roughly in the order I see families reach for them.

**Savings and the estate.** The person who signs the contract with the funeral home is responsible for the bill. The estate normally reimburses that person, but an estate can take months to settle. If most of the money is in an IRA or a house, the family still has to find cash in the meantime. One good reason to plan ahead is to make sure somebody can pay the bill quickly without draining their own accounts.

**Life insurance and final expense insurance.** A life insurance policy pays a tax-free death benefit to a named beneficiary, usually within weeks of a claim, without going through probate. **Final expense insurance** is a smaller whole life policy built for this specific job: a benefit sized to cover a funeral, a last medical bill or two and the small debts that come due right after a death. Many are available into the eighties with a few health questions rather than an exam. Some issue with no health questions at all, and those usually pay a reduced benefit if death occurs in the first two years.

This is one of the lines the practice sells alongside Medicare, and it is not right for everyone. Plenty of retirees with a sizable portfolio or an existing paid-up life policy already have the cash covered. Whether a separate policy makes sense depends on how liquid your assets are, who would be signing the funeral contract, and whether you want that money kept apart from everything else. I won't quote premiums or policies in an article. A licensed professional can price it against your age and health.

**A prepaid funeral agreement.** Arizona law sets specific protections for prepaid ("prearranged") funeral agreements funded by a trust, and they are worth knowing before you sign one:

- **The money goes into a trust account** at a financial institution doing business in Arizona, generally within five business days. It stays the property of the beneficiary during their lifetime and of the estate afterward (A.R.S. § 32-1391.05).
- **The funeral home's initial service fee is capped at 15%** of the total amount of the agreement (A.R.S. § 32-1391.06).
- **You can cancel.** Within three business days of signing you get a full refund, including the service fee. After that, a written request gets you the remaining trust funds back within five business days (A.R.S. § 32-1391.07).

Some prepaid agreements are funded by an insurance policy rather than a trust, and different rules apply. Ask which kind you are being offered. Also think about whether you might move: a north Peoria couple who may spend their last years near a child in another state should read the transfer terms closely.

**Veterans benefits, close to home.** North Phoenix is home to a VA national cemetery. The **National Memorial Cemetery of Arizona** sits off Cave Creek Road at Pinnacle Peak Road in ZIP 85024, a short drive down I-17 from Anthem and a little longer from north Peoria. For an eligible veteran, the VA provides the gravesite, opening and closing of the grave, a government headstone or marker and perpetual care at no cost. A spouse can generally be buried in the same gravesite, even if the spouse was not a veteran.

The VA also pays **burial allowances** in some cases: up to $2,000 when the death is service-connected, and smaller amounts for some non-service-connected deaths. Those depend on the circumstances, so check with the VA. The funeral home's own charges for the service are still the family's to pay. Many Peoria households have a veteran in them, often from Luke Air Force Base, and pre-registering eligibility with the VA is one of the most useful steps you can take now.

## How to keep the bill in check

You do not need a dollar figure from me to know a funeral costs thousands of dollars, and the spread between a direct cremation and a traditional burial is wide. Cremation is now the majority choice nationally: the National Funeral Directors Association projected a 63.4% cremation rate for 2025. Two rules protect you whichever you choose:

- **Ask for the General Price List.** The FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453) requires a funeral home to give you an itemized price list when you ask about arrangements in person. You can buy only the goods and services you want, rather than a package, and the home must accept a casket or urn you bought elsewhere.
- **Compare two or three funeral homes.** Prices across the Valley vary a lot for the same service. Calling around is easier to do now, calmly, than on the worst day of your year.

## The short version

- **Medicare pays nothing toward a funeral, burial or cremation.** Neither does Medicare Advantage, Medigap or Part D. Hospice ends at death.
- **Social Security pays a one-time $255** to a surviving spouse in the same household, or in some cases an eligible child. Nobody else.
- **What actually pays:** savings and the estate, life or final expense insurance, an Arizona prepaid funeral trust, and veterans benefits at the national cemetery in North Phoenix.
- **Arizona prepaid trusts are refundable:** in full within three business days, and the remaining trust balance after that.
- **Ask for the General Price List** and compare more than one funeral home.
