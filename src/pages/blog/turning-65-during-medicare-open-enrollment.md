**If you are turning 65, you enroll during your own seven-month Initial Enrollment Period, not during the October 15 to December 7 open enrollment you are seeing advertised.** Open enrollment is for people who already have Medicare. But if your 65th birthday falls between November 2026 and March 2027, the two windows overlap on the calendar, and that is where the confusion starts. Turn 65 in January 2027 and your window opened October 1, 2026, closes April 30, 2027, and any sign-up by December 31 starts Part A and Part B on January 1.

I am getting this question a lot from north Peoria right now, often from Vistancia or Trilogy households where one spouse is already on Medicare and is shopping the fall menu, and the other turns 65 in January. The couple sits down at the same kitchen table with the same mailers, but they are working on two different clocks. Here is how to keep them straight.

## Which window is yours?

Two different federal rules are running at once this fall.

- **Your Initial Enrollment Period (IEP)** is set by your birthday. It runs from three months before your birthday month to three months after it. This is when you sign up for Part A and Part B, and it opens your first chance to pick a Medicare Advantage plan or a Part D drug plan.
- **The Annual Enrollment Period (AEP)**, October 15 through December 7, is for people already on Medicare to change what they have. Anything chosen in it starts January 1.

For someone new to Medicare, the plan-choice window has its own name too: the **Initial Coverage Election Period**. Under [42 CFR §422.62](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/422.62) it begins three months before the month you have both Part A and Part B. For most people that is the same day the IEP opens. It does not close on December 7.

So the plain answer for a January birthday: you are not "using open enrollment." You are using your own window, which happens to sit on top of it.

## Your dates if you turn 65 between November and March

This is the stretch where the two windows overlap. The coverage start assumes you sign up in the three months before your birthday month.

If you sign up in your birthday month or later, you are not penalized, but coverage starts the first of the month after you enroll. If your birthday is on the **1st**, Medicare treats you as turning 65 the previous month, so shift every date in your row up by one. A January 1 birthday is a December row.

The [enrollment timeline tool](/tools/enrollment-timeline/) works out your exact dates from your birthday if you would rather not count months.

## The trap: shopping last year's plan by mistake

The mailers landing in Peoria right now advertise 2027 plans, and anything you see on Medicare.gov's plan finder this month defaults to 2027. That is good news for a January, February or March birthday, because 2027 is the year you are buying.

It is a real trap for **November and December birthdays.** If your coverage starts November 1 or December 1, the plan you join is a 2026 plan for those first weeks. On January 1 it becomes whatever that plan looks like in 2027, which could mean a new premium, a new drug list or a different network. In Maricopa County, several 2026 plans do not continue into 2027 at all. ([We listed them here.](/blog/medicare-advantage-plans-ending-arizona-2027/)) Ask for the 2027 version of any plan before you join it in November or December. Once you are enrolled you are on Medicare like everyone else, so the regular open enrollment through December 7 is open to you too.

## Two second chances, and one that does not come back

New enrollees get more room than they usually realize, with one important exception.

**If you join a Medicare Advantage plan and it turns out wrong,** §422.62(a)(3)(ii) gives a new enrollee one change during the first three months of coverage, either to another Advantage plan or back to Original Medicare. For a January 1 start that runs through March 31, 2027, the same weeks as the general Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period.

**If you miss your IEP entirely,** the General Enrollment Period runs January 1 to March 31 each year, with Part B starting the month after you sign up. The late-enrollment penalty can follow you for life, though, so this is a backstop, not a plan.

**The Medicare Supplement window is the one that does not come back.** Your six-month Medigap Open Enrollment starts the month your Part B starts and you are 65. During those six months, an Arizona insurer has to sell you any Medigap plan it offers, without health questions. For a January 2027 start it runs through June 30, 2027. Arizona has **no birthday rule** and no annual Medigap switching right, so after June 30 a move into Plan G or Plan N usually means medical underwriting. ([More on Arizona's Medigap rules.](/blog/when-is-medicare-supplement-open-enrollment-arizona/)) If you are leaning toward a Supplement, or even unsure, make that decision inside the six months.

## The north Peoria angle: where your doctors are

Most of my Peoria clients turning 65 live in the 85383 corridor and see doctors who are not. Primary care is often near Lake Pleasant Parkway, and the specialists are toward Arrowhead or out in Sun City. Medicare Advantage networks in Maricopa County are drawn around hospital systems, and plans that look identical in a mailer can split on whether your cardiologist in Sun City is in or out.

So before choosing anything, check each doctor by name, not the clinic, not the group. That matters even more this year, when [plans are being discontinued in Maricopa](/blog/medicare-advantage-plan-discontinued-what-to-do-peoria-az-2027/) and the line-up has shifted. A Supplement with Original Medicare avoids the network question entirely, which is why the six-month Medigap window above matters so much to people with specialists spread across the West Valley. ## A checklist for a January–March 2027 birthday, starting this week

1. **Confirm your dates.** Count from your birthday, and remember the 1st-of-the-month shift. Write down the last day to enroll for coverage on the 1st of your birthday month.
2. **Sign up for Part A and Part B** at [Social Security](https://www.ssa.gov/medicare/sign-up) if you are not already drawing benefits. If you are, your card comes automatically. If you are still working with group coverage, [read this first](/blog/do-i-need-medicare-at-65-if-still-working/).
3. **List your prescriptions, pharmacy and doctors.** This takes most of the time, and it decides most of the outcome.
4. **Compare 2027 plans only.** Supplement plus Part D, or Medicare Advantage, priced and network-checked for 2027.
5. **Decide before your Medigap six months close,** even if you choose Advantage, so it is a choice and not a default.
6. **Do not let a spouse's December 7 deadline become yours.** If your partner is already on Medicare and changing plans, theirs is the AEP deadline. Yours is set by your birthday.
