**No. Using a Medicare agent or broker in Arizona does not cost you anything, and it does not change your premium.** The insurance company pays the agent. A Medicare Advantage plan must charge every member the same premium under federal rules, and Arizona's anti-rebating law, [A.R.S. 20-449](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/20/00449.htm), names Medicare Advantage, Medicare Supplement and Part D plans in its definitions. The price is the same whether you enroll through an agent, the insurer's own website or Medicare.gov.

I get this question most from people turning 65 in North Peoria, the 85383 end around Vistancia and Lake Pleasant Parkway, who have just moved from a state where they bought health insurance through work and never met an agent. It is a fair thing to ask before you let someone into your kitchen with a stack of forms, so here is how the money actually works.

## Who pays the agent

The plan you pick pays the agent. Nothing comes out of your pocket, and nothing is added to your bill.

- **Medicare Advantage and Part D.** Federal rules limit what the insurer may pay. Under [42 CFR 422.2274](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/422.2274), plans are held to compensation amounts CMS sets, and CMS publishes the [compensation data](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/health-plans/managedcaremarketing/agentbroker) every year. Agents are paid only for the months you stay enrolled, and if you leave a new plan in the first three months the agent generally has to pay the commission back.
- **Medicare Supplement (Medigap).** The insurer pays the commission out of the premiums it collects. Medigap is regulated by the state rather than by CMS's compensation table, and your premium is the company's rate for your plan letter, based on factors like your age, ZIP code and tobacco use. The rate does not change because an agent submitted the application.
- **Part A and Part B.** No one is paid for these. You sign up through Social Security yourself, and the standard Part B premium is {{partB.premium}} a month in {{figYear}} whoever helps you.

## Why the price is the same either way

Two rules keep it level, one federal and one Arizona.

**Medicare Advantage premiums are uniform.** [42 CFR 422.262(c)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/422.262) says a plan's premiums "may not vary among individuals enrolled in an MA plan." So a $0 plan is $0, and a plan with a premium charges the same amount to the person who enrolled online and the person who sat down with me.

**Arizona bars rebates.** A.R.S. 20-449 forbids any "rebate of premiums payable on the contract" as an inducement on disability insurance, which in Arizona includes health coverage ([A.R.S. 20-253](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/20/00253.htm)). Subsection C spells out Medicare Advantage, Medicare Supplement and Part D plans by name. So no agent can lawfully "beat" the price either. Shopping by agent does not change the number. Shopping by plan and company does.

One wrinkle is worth knowing at 65. Arizona law ([A.R.S. 20-1133](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/20/01133.htm)) lets a Medigap insurer file rates that include an **early enrollment discount**. Some companies price new 65-year-olds that way and some do not, so the same Plan G can cost noticeably different amounts at two companies. That is a reason to compare companies, and it comes from the company, not from whoever writes the application.

Here is the local reason the middle column matters. According to CMS's 2027 plan landscape files, Maricopa County has **45 general Medicare Advantage drug plans from 13 organizations** for 2027, not counting Special Needs Plans. A captive agent, or an insurer's own call centre, can walk you through one of those 13. That can be fine if you already know the company you want. If you don't, you are comparing one shelf of a store with 13 shelves.

It also matters this year because the menu moved. Several Valley plans ended for 2027 and new ones arrived (see [which Arizona plans are ending](/blog/medicare-advantage-plans-ending-arizona-2027/)). A 65-year-old in Peoria choosing for the first time is choosing from a different list than a neighbor who signed up two years ago.

## Agent rules that protect you

Medicare puts rules on agents that are worth knowing, whoever you work with.

- **A Scope of Appointment comes first.** Before a sales meeting about Medicare Advantage or Part D, the agent has to agree with you on what products you will discuss and record it, in writing for an in-person meeting ([42 CFR 422.2264(c)(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/422.2264)). If you asked about a Medigap plan, you should not be getting an Advantage pitch.
- **Sales calls are recorded.** Marketing and sales calls through third-party marketing organizations must be recorded in full and kept for six years (42 CFR 422.2274(g)(2)(ii)).
- **No consulting fees.** Medicare Advantage plans must make sure you are not charged marketing consulting fees while you are considering enrollment (42 CFR 422.2274(c)(8)).
- **Licensing.** Anyone enrolling you has to be licensed in Arizona and appointed with the company. You can ask for an agent's license number and check it with the Arizona Department of Insurance and Financial Institutions.

## Five questions to ask any agent

1. **How many companies can you enroll me with**, for Medicare Supplement and for Advantage? Get a number, not "most of them."
2. **Are you paid differently depending on which plan I choose?** An honest agent will tell you how compensation works.
3. **Will you check my doctors and prescriptions** against every plan you show me, by name?
4. **Will you be the one I call next fall** when my plan's Annual Notice of Change arrives?
5. **Is there anything I will pay you directly?** The answer should be no.

If you would rather not use an agent at all, that is a real option. Arizona runs a free, state-run Medicare counseling program with no plans to sell, and Medicare.gov's plan finder lists every Advantage and Part D plan in your ZIP.

## What this means if you turn 65 soon

The thing that costs money at 65 is not the agent. It is missing a window. Your one-time Medigap open enrollment, the six months that start the month your Part B begins, is the only time an Arizona insurer must sell you any Medigap plan without health questions, because Arizona has no birthday rule ([details here](/blog/when-is-medicare-supplement-open-enrollment-arizona/)).
