**No. Medicare does not pay for a coronary calcium score when it is done as a screening test, which is how it is almost always ordered. Original Medicare covers screening only where the law names the test, and this one is not named, so most people on Medicare pay the imaging center's cash price, typically $100 to $150.** What Medicare does cover for your heart is a screening blood panel every five years and, once there is a symptom or a diagnosis, the diagnostic tests a cardiologist orders.

The scan itself is quick. It is a low-dose CT of the chest, no contrast, no IV, about ten minutes on the table, and it gives your doctor a number for how much calcified plaque sits in the coronary arteries. Doctors use it to decide whether a statin makes sense for someone with borderline risk. That is why it comes up so often in north Peoria. Vistancia and Trilogy are full of active people in their late sixties with cholesterol in the gray zone, and the calcium score is the tiebreaker their doctor suggests.

## Why Medicare does not pay for it

Medicare's starting rule, in section 1862(a) of the Social Security Act, is that it pays for care that is reasonable and necessary to diagnose or treat an illness or injury. Screening, meaning a test on someone with no symptoms, is the exception, and Congress has added screening benefits one at a time: mammograms, colonoscopies, the cholesterol panel, lung cancer CT for heavy smokers and so on. A coronary calcium score has never been added.

So the scan falls back under the general rule. For a person with no chest pain, no shortness of breath and no known heart disease, it is screening, and Medicare's contractors treat it as not covered. The coverage policy most often cited, LCD L35121, says so directly, and adds that when a calcium score is done during a covered CT angiogram, it does not get any separate payment.

There is no Arizona exception. A few states have passed laws making private insurers cover the scan, but those laws reach employer and individual policies, not Medicare. A Peoria retiree on Medicare is under the same federal rule as everyone else.

The third row is the important one. If you have symptoms, the conversation is different. A cardiologist who is working up chest discomfort will usually order a covered test such as a stress test or a CT angiogram, not a stand-alone calcium score, and Part B pays its share. The cholesterol panel and the annual visit also show up in your [yearly wellness visit](/blog/does-medicare-cover-an-annual-physical-peoria-az/), which is where most people first hear about the calcium scan.

## What a calcium score costs out of pocket

GoodRx puts the typical cash price at about $100 to $150, with some places below $100 and some hospital departments at $400 or more. Freestanding imaging centers, the kind along Lake Pleasant Parkway and over toward Arrowhead, tend to sit at the low end. Hospital outpatient departments tend to sit at the top.

Three habits keep the bill predictable:

- **Ask for the self-pay price by name.** "Cardiac CT calcium scoring, self-pay" gets you a number. Without that, the center may run it through Medicare first.
- **Expect an Advance Beneficiary Notice.** Because Medicare is expected to deny the scan, many centers will have you sign a form saying you know that and agree to pay. That is normal. Read the price on it.
- **Get a copy of the report for your own file.** The score is a number you will want to hand to any new doctor, especially if you split the year between Peoria and a summer place.

## Does a Medicare Supplement or Advantage plan change the answer?

**A Medicare Supplement does not.** Plan G, Plan N and the rest pay their share only on claims Medicare approves. A scan Medicare denies is a scan the supplement denies too. Supplements are the strongest way to cover the heart care that *is* covered, the stent, the cardiac rehab and the specialist visits, but they do not reach past Medicare's list. (If rehab is where you are headed, see [what Medicare pays for cardiac rehab in Peoria](/blog/does-medicare-cover-cardiac-rehab-peoria-az/).)

**A Medicare Advantage plan usually does not.** A [Medicare Advantage](/medicare-advantage/) plan has to cover everything Original Medicare covers, and it can add extras. Some plans add a calcium score as a supplemental benefit, but it is not a common one. If you are on one, look in the Evidence of Coverage under diagnostic radiology and supplemental benefits, or call the member number on the card and ask for the answer in writing. If the plan does cover it, it will also tell you which imaging centers are in network and whether it needs approval first.

## The Peoria picture

Heart disease is common enough here that this is not an unusual question. CDC's PLACES estimates put coronary heart disease at roughly one adult in twenty across Maricopa County, and diagnosed high blood pressure at close to three in ten. Those are adult-wide figures, and heart disease gets more common with age, so among retirees in Peoria's 85383 corridor and Sun City next door the share runs higher.

Two local patterns are worth knowing:

- **Concierge practices recommend it more often.** A lot of north Peoria and Trilogy residents pay a retainer for a concierge doctor, and those practices often suggest a calcium score as part of a longer annual workup. The retainer does not make Medicare pay for the scan. I covered what Medicare does and does not pay in a concierge arrangement in [Does Medicare cover concierge medicine in Arizona?](/blog/does-medicare-cover-concierge-medicine-arizona/)
- **Your HSA can pay for it.** People who worked into their late sixties often reach Medicare with money still in a health savings account. You cannot contribute once Medicare starts, but you can spend the balance tax free on qualified medical expenses, and a doctor-recommended scan is one. That is a better use of the account than the cash in your wallet. The rules for that transition are in [Can I contribute to an HSA while on Medicare?](/blog/can-i-contribute-to-an-hsa-while-on-medicare/)

## The short version

- **Medicare does not cover a screening calcium score.** The scan is not on the list of screenings Congress has added, and that holds in Arizona too.
- **Expect to pay cash, typically $100 to $150**, more at a hospital. Ask for the self-pay price.
- **Use what Medicare does cover:** the cholesterol panel every five years and the yearly heart-health visit, both $0 with a provider who accepts assignment.
- **Symptoms change the answer.** A test ordered because something is wrong is diagnostic, and Part B covers it.
- **Supplements will not pay for it, and few Advantage plans do.** If you are on an Advantage plan, check the Evidence of Coverage before you book.
