**On November 25, 2026, CMS published its proposed rule for the 2028 Medicare Advantage and Part D plan year (CMS-4214-P2, Federal Register document 2026-23960). It is a proposal, not law. Comments close January 19, 2027, and most of it would apply to plans starting January 1, 2028. The change members would notice most: if a doctor or hospital leaves your Advantage network, you would keep paying in-network prices with them for at least 90 days. The rule would also cap what a plan can charge for an emergency room visit or a ground ambulance ride, and it would start a mandatory new payment model in 2029 in a randomly chosen group of states.**

None of this touches the plan you are choosing for {{nextYear}}. Those plans, premiums and networks were set in September. What the rule shows is which way Medicare Advantage is heading. If you live in north Peoria and use an Advantage HMO, the doctor-leaves piece applies to you directly, because a network split is exactly what happened here this year.

## The five proposals a member would notice

The rule runs 274 pages in the Federal Register. Most of it covers plan bids, data reporting and how CMS oversees insurers. These are the parts that change what a member gets, pays or receives in the mail:

Three more change what you see without changing your coverage. The Star Ratings would drop to 15 measures, and the "reward factor" that boosts consistently high scorers would go away. The Disaster/Emergency special enrollment period would go only through 1-800-MEDICARE, with CMS approving each use. And CMS is asking for comments on bringing back automatic "seamless" enrollment, which would move someone turning 65 from a carrier's under-65 plan into the same carrier's Advantage plan unless they choose something else. That last one is only a request for comments so far. It is not a proposal yet.

## Why the 90-day rule matters in north Peoria

North Peoria and North Phoenix already show the problem this proposal is meant to fix. HonorHealth's primary care doctors left the AZ Blue Best Life (HMO) network on July 1, 2026, and its specialists and hospitals leave on January 1, 2027. That includes HonorHealth Sonoran Crossing, off I-17 at Dove Valley Road, which plenty of north Peoria and Anthem households use. The [HonorHealth and AZ Blue post](/blog/is-honorhealth-leaving-az-blue-medicare-advantage-2027/) covers the details. Under current rules, members who stayed on that plan and kept seeing an HonorHealth specialist would owe out-of-network prices from January 1, unless AZ Blue grants an individual continuity-of-care request.

The proposal would make that cushion automatic: 90 days of coverage at in-network cost sharing, starting on the termination date, for anyone who has seen that provider in the past 12 months. That covers the follow-up after a procedure or the next round of a treatment plan. It is enough time to move your records to a new in-network doctor without paying out-of-network bills while you do.

There is a trade-off, and CMS says so in the rule. The notice would arrive **after** the doctor leaves, not before. CMS's reasoning is that most contract fights settle at the last minute, and early letters lead people to switch doctors or plans over a split that never happens. That is fair. But you would also lose the 30 to 45 days of warning you get now. If you are mid-treatment with one specialist, the habit I already recommend matters more under this rule: every fall, look up that specialist by name in the new plan's directory, and call the office to confirm. The [doctor-leaves-mid-year post](/blog/what-if-my-doctor-leaves-my-medicare-advantage-network/) walks through how.

## ER and ambulance limits, and the cost that could shift elsewhere

An ambulance ride from Vistancia to Banner Del E. Webb in Sun City West or to Abrazo Arrowhead is a long, expensive trip on a plan with a high ambulance copay. CMS's own numbers make the case for a limit: an average of $275 per ground ambulance trip in Medicare Advantage, against about $120 under Original Medicare's 20 percent on 2027 projections.

The rule is also open about the catch. Plans have to price their benefits to equal Original Medicare overall, so a plan forced to lower one copay "may increase cost sharing for other benefits" to balance its bid. So check the whole plan, not one line of the summary. The [out-of-pocket maximum](/blog/medicare-advantage-out-of-pocket-maximum-peoria-az-2027/) still limits the damage in a bad year, and that is the number to compare first.

## The grocery card and the "Giveback" plans

Many Maricopa plans advertise a grocery or "healthy food" allowance on a debit card. The proposal would require everything bought with it to meet a CMS healthy-food standard based on the federal Dietary Guidelines, unless a doctor recommends the item for your condition. This matters most if a card was your reason for picking a plan. The amount is set each year, and in 2028 it would buy a narrower list of items. This rule does not touch the Part B giveback, which lowers your {{partB.premium}} premium. That benefit is a separate thing.

## Could Arizona be an ADVaNCE MA state?

This is the biggest structural change in the rule, and nobody can answer it yet. ADVaNCE MA would be the first **mandatory** Medicare Advantage model from the CMS Innovation Center. In the states selected, Advantage plans would be paid and rated under an alternative quality program, would be allowed to target certain supplemental benefits, and would have a new risk-adjustment formula tested in the background. It would run for 10 years, January 1, 2029 through December 31, 2038.

CMS would sort the 50 states and D.C. into seven groups and pick at random within each one. Territories, and states with fewer than 1,000 people in eligible plans, would be left out. Arizona clears that bar easily. According to CMS's 2027 landscape files, Maricopa County alone has 45 general Advantage plans with drug coverage from 13 insurers, and every one of them would fall under the model if Arizona is picked. The state list comes out with the final rule. Until then, anyone who says Arizona is or isn't in the model is guessing.

## What to do with this now

For {{nextYear}} coverage, nothing in this rule changes your choice. The dates that matter are the ones already on the calendar:

- **December 7, 2026:** Annual Enrollment closes for a January 1 start.
- **February 28, 2027:** last day to pick a new Medicare Advantage or Part D plan if your plan is ending. Choose in January and it starts February 1; choose in February and it starts March 1. If yours is one of the [Arizona plans ending for 2027](/blog/medicare-advantage-plans-ending-arizona-2027/), the [discontinued-plan steps](/blog/medicare-advantage-plan-discontinued-what-to-do-peoria-az-2027/) apply.
- **January 1 – March 31, 2027:** if you are already on an Advantage plan, you can make one switch to another Advantage plan or back to Original Medicare.
- **January 19, 2027:** last day to comment on the 2028 rule. Anyone can comment at regulations.gov under docket CMS-4214-P2. If you have had a doctor leave your network partway through a treatment, CMS has asked specifically whether 90 days is the right length.

CMS usually publishes the final rule in the spring. That is when we will know what survived, and which states are in the model.
