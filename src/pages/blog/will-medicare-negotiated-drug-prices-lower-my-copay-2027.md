**Medicare's negotiated prices lower your copay in 2027 only if your plan charges a percentage of the drug's price, or if the drug falls inside your deductible.** On January 1, 2027, negotiated prices take effect for 15 Part D drugs, among them Ozempic, Trelegy Ellipta, Janumet and Xtandi. According to CMS, they run 38% to 85% below the drugs' 2024 list prices. That is the price your plan pays. If your plan charges you a flat copay for the drug, a lower price behind the counter may not change what you hand the pharmacist.

CMS estimates people with Part D will save about $685 million in out-of-pocket costs in the first year. That is real money, but it is spread unevenly. Some people will see their monthly cost for an inhaler fall by more than a hundred dollars. Others, on the same drug in a different plan, will see no change at all.

I am writing this for the household in north Peoria, say off Lake Pleasant Parkway near Happy Valley Road, that read the headline and wants to know whether it changes anything about the plan they pick before December 7. The answer depends on three things in your plan's 2027 paperwork, and you can check all three in about ten minutes.

CMS says about 5.3 million people with Part D used at least one of these drugs in 2024. Ozempic and its sister products alone accounted for 2.3 million of them, and Trelegy for 1.3 million. The first round, ten drugs including Eliquis and Jardiance, took effect on January 1 of this year. CMS published a final rule codifying the program in the Federal Register on November 9, 2026. It does not change the 2027 prices above.

Wegovy appears on the list, but Part D still excludes drugs used only for weight loss. The negotiated price helps a Wegovy user only when the drug is covered for a medically accepted condition. My [post on weight-loss drugs](/blog/does-medicare-cover-weight-loss-drugs-arizona/) covers that line.

## Why a price cut does not always reach the counter

The negotiated price, which the law calls the maximum fair price, is what the drug costs your plan. CMS's guidance requires the price your plan uses to calculate your share to reflect that negotiated price. What your share is depends on how your plan charges for the drug, and plans use two methods.

- **Coinsurance** is a percentage of the drug's price, such as 25%. When the price falls, your share falls in step. This is where the savings are most visible.
- **A flat copay** is a fixed dollar amount per fill. It was set when the plan built its 2027 formulary, and it does not move because the drug got cheaper. If your plan charged a $47 copay for a drug in 2026 and charges $47 in 2027, the negotiated price did nothing for you at the counter.

Most plans use flat copays on their generic tiers and coinsurance on higher brand and specialty tiers, but the line differs plan by plan. You have to look.

Here is a rough illustration for Trelegy, using CMS's 2024 list price as a stand-in for what the pharmacy charged before. At 25% coinsurance, a month's supply cost about $163.50. At the same 25% on the 2027 negotiated price of $175, it costs $43.75. Over a year that is roughly $1,962 against $525. The same inhaler under a flat-copay plan costs whatever the copay says, in both years.

## The deductible is where the drop shows up first

When a drug falls inside your Part D deductible, you pay the plan's price for it until the deductible is met. For these 15 drugs that price is now built on the negotiated price. A drug that used to clear most of the deductible in one January fill now takes several months to work through it. The deductible itself is no smaller. For 2027 a plan can charge up to $700, up from {{partD.maxDeductible}} in {{year}}. You pay it in smaller pieces, and the drop shows up as a lower pharmacy bill in January.

Here is the local fact that makes this matter in the Valley. I counted the deductibles in CMS's 2027 plan landscape files:

- **Only 3 of the 45 general Medicare Advantage plans with drug coverage in Maricopa County have no drug deductible for 2027.** That is down from 6 of 46 in 2026.
- **12 of those 45 charge the full $700.**
- **6 of Arizona's 9 standalone drug plans charge the full $700**, and none of the nine has a $0 deductible. In 2026 one did.

Many plans exempt their generic tiers from the deductible, so the deductible usually bites on brand-name drugs, and that includes most of the 15. Put that together and more Maricopa members than last year will meet one of these drugs inside the deductible in January. The negotiated price reaches that part of the year most directly.

## Specialty drugs: the cap still sets the total

For the cancer and rare-disease drugs on the list (Xtandi, Pomalyst, Ibrance, Calquence, Ofev and Austedo), the negotiated price is still several thousand dollars a month. Anyone taking one of them reaches the Part D out-of-pocket cap early in the year with or without negotiation. The cap is $2,400 for 2027, up from {{partD.cap}} in {{year}}, and once you reach it you pay nothing more for covered drugs that calendar year.

So for a north Peoria retiree on Xtandi through an oncologist near Arrowhead, the negotiation changes the timing, not the total. At the lower price it may take two fills instead of one to reach the cap. The year still costs $2,400 out of pocket, which is $300 more than this year because the cap itself rose. If spreading that over the year helps, the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan does it. My [post on the 2027 cap](/blog/medicare-part-d-out-of-pocket-cap-2027/) explains what counts toward it.

## How to check your own drug before December 7

Open Enrollment closes December 7, three weeks from today. If you take one of the 15 drugs, check these three things in your plan's 2027 Annual Notice of Change or Evidence of Coverage, or in Medicare's Plan Finder:

1. **The tier the drug sits on for 2027.** Plans must cover the negotiated drugs, but each plan picks the tier. A drug can move tiers from one year to the next.
2. **Whether that tier charges coinsurance or a flat copay.** Coinsurance means the price cut reaches you. A flat copay means it probably does not.
3. **Whether the tier is subject to the deductible, and how large the deductible is.** A brand drug behind a $700 deductible will cost you the negotiated price each month until the deductible is met.

Also check for **prior authorization or step therapy** on the drug. A lower price does not help if the plan makes you try something else first. If the drug is a GLP-1, check whether the plan covers it for your diagnosis.

When two plans in the Peoria area cover your drugs on comparable terms, the negotiated price can tip the choice toward the one that charges coinsurance. That is especially true for an inhaler or a diabetes drug you fill every month. When they differ on networks, which matters a great deal along the Peoria–Glendale–Sun City seam, the network usually matters more than the drug price.
