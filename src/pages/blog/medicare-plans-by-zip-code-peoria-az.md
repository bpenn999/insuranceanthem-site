**Mostly, no — and the part people worry about is the part that does not change.** Medicare Advantage and Part D plans are approved county by county, so 85345 down by Grand Avenue and 85383 up in Vistancia are generally offered the same list of plans. Your neighbor did not get a secret menu.

What your address does decide is which of those plans is any good *for you*: how far each network reaches from your house, which Peoria and Arrowhead doctors are inside it, and what a Medicare Supplement costs. And there is one Peoria address type where the whole menu really does change — the north end of the city, past Lake Pleasant, is in a different county altogether.

## Do Medicare plans change by ZIP code?

The plan finder asks for your ZIP code, which is where the assumption comes from. What it does with it is look up your **county**, because that is the unit Medicare Advantage and Part D service areas are actually drawn in. An insurer applies for approval county by county, and it only gets that approval where it can show an adequate local provider network.

So inside Maricopa County, from Peoria to Glendale to Anthem, the list of plans is largely one list. Two caveats worth knowing rather than guessing at:

- A plan **can** file a partial-county service area, in which case it really is offered in some ZIPs and not others. It is uncommon in a metro county like this one, but it is the reason to check your own ZIP in the plan finder rather than assume.
- **Premiums, copays and extras are set for the whole service area** — so when two people in the same county are quoted different numbers for the same plan, the usual explanation is that one of them is looking at a different plan year, a different plan from the same company, or an IRMAA surcharge on their Part B rather than anything to do with the plan.

Nationally, the average person with Medicare could choose among 32 Medicare Advantage drug plans for 2026, and roughly 28% of beneficiaries live somewhere offering more than 50. Maricopa County is firmly at the crowded end of that range. That abundance is genuinely useful and it is also the whole problem — a long list is not a short list of *suitable* ones.

## Peoria sits in two counties, and that is where the answer flips

This is the local detail almost nobody accounts for. **Peoria is a city in Maricopa and Yavapai counties.** Most of it — all the built-out neighborhoods, from the 85345 core through Westbrook Village and up into the Vistancia corridor — is Maricopa. But the city limits run north past Lake Pleasant, and that northern portion is in Yavapai County.

If your permanent residence is on the Yavapai side, then:

- your Medicare Advantage and Part D options are **Yavapai County's list**, not Maricopa's, and the two are not the same;
- the plan finder will get this right from your ZIP even when your mailing address, your instincts and your neighbors all say "Peoria";
- and a plan a friend three miles south is happy with may not be sold to you at all.

The same logic applies in the other direction along the **Peoria–Glendale–Sun City seam**, where ZIPs sit close enough together that people compare notes across a street. Within Maricopa County the plan list holds steady there — but the hospital systems each network is built around do not, which brings us to the thing that actually matters.

## What really changes between 85345 and 85383

Peoria runs close to thirty miles north to south, and the Medicare conversation is genuinely different at each end. The north 85383 corridor is newer, with a lot of recent arrivals still holding a plan they bought in another state. The 85345 core and Westbrook Village have households who have used the same doctor for twenty years and have no intention of stopping.

Both of those people can enroll in the identical plan. It will not perform identically.

- **North Peoria** residents generally drive to Arrowhead, or into Sun City, for specialist care. Whether a given plan's network reaches those specific practices from up there is the deciding question, and it is not answered by the plan's star rating or its brochure.
- **South and central Peoria** are close to a denser cluster of primary care and hospital options, so networks tend to be less of a constraint — but a twenty-year relationship with one physician is a hard thing to trade for a lower copay.

That is why the useful comparison is never "which plan is better in Peoria". It is: *type your own doctors and your own prescriptions into your own ZIP, and see what survives.* [How Medicare Advantage networks are actually drawn](/learn/medicare-advantage-networks/) covers the mechanics, and the [Peoria service-area page](/service-area/peoria-az/) has the local version. If you are on the Glendale side of the seam, [that page](/service-area/glendale-az/) is the one.

If your Part B premium is higher than your neighbor's, the cause is almost always IRMAA and almost never geography. You can [check where your income lands](/tools/irmaa-estimator/) in about a minute, and if a one-off event pushed you over — a home sale, a Roth conversion, a retirement — that is appealable on Form SSA-44 and worth a conversation with your tax advisor.

## Medigap is where your ZIP genuinely shows up in the price

Medicare Supplements are standardized by letter, so the coverage question is settled before you shop: a Plan G is a Plan G. The price is not settled at all. Each company files its own rates and uses location as one of the inputs, so the same lettered plan can be quoted differently in 85345 than in 85383 — and much more importantly, differently by company for the same person at the same address.

Two things matter more than the ZIP:

1. **Your six-month Medigap Open Enrollment**, which starts the month your Part B begins. Inside it, no company can decline you or price you up for your health history. **Arizona does not have a birthday rule**, so once that window closes, medical underwriting is generally back on the table here. Some other states allow an annual switch; this one does not, and I would rather you heard that now than in year three.
2. **Whether a supplement suits how you live.** A lot of the 55-plus communities in Peoria — Westbrook Village, Trilogy at Vistancia — have households who spend four or five months a year somewhere else. Original Medicare plus a supplement has no network anywhere in the country, which is often the better structural fit for that life than a plan built around a Maricopa County network. [The comparison in full](/learn/medicare-advantage-vs-medigap/) lays out both sides.

## How to price your own address instead of your neighbor's

Fifteen minutes, in this order:

1. **Write down your prescriptions with dosages, your pharmacy, and the doctors you will not give up.** Every honest comparison starts here, and none can be done without it.
2. **Use your own ZIP, and your own permanent address** — the one Social Security has on file. A winter place does not give you a second plan menu, and if you are anywhere north of Lake Pleasant, confirm which county you are actually in before you shop.
3. **Check each shortlisted plan's network against your doctors by name**, not by hospital system. Systems and insurers renegotiate; a practice can be in the network while the specialist you were referred to is not.
4. **Get supplement quotes from more than one company** if you are inside your Medigap open enrollment, because that spread is the real money.
5. If you would rather narrow it before shopping, the [plan type finder](/tools/plan-type-finder/) asks six questions and points you toward one of the two routes.
