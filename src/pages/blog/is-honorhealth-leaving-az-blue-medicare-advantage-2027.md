**Yes. HonorHealth says that as of January 1, 2027 it will no longer be in network with the AZ Blue Best Life (HMO) Medicare Advantage plans, and that covers its employed physicians, its specialists and its hospitals, including HonorHealth Sonoran Crossing and HonorHealth Deer Valley in North Phoenix. Its primary care doctors came out of that network earlier, on July 1, 2026. The plans are not ending: Blue Best Life Classic and Plus are in the CMS 2027 file for Maricopa, Pinal and Pima counties, a new Select plan joins them, and about 31,600 Maricopa County households were on them in September. HonorHealth says the change applies only to Best Life (HMO) plans and that it stays in network with every other Blue Cross Blue Shield of Arizona plan.**

I am writing this for the north end of my patch, North Phoenix, Anthem and north Peoria, because those are the households for whom HonorHealth is the hospital system down the freeway. HonorHealth Sonoran Crossing Medical Center sits at Interstate 17 and Dove Valley Road, and HonorHealth Deer Valley Medical Center is at the I-17 and Loop 101 interchange on 27th Avenue. For a Blue Best Life member in 85085, 85086 or the Happy Valley corridor, those two buildings and the HonorHealth doctors who admit to them are the practical meaning of this notice. A plan continuing and a plan still working for you are different things, and this is a case where the first is true and the second may not be.

## What HonorHealth says is changing, and when

Everything below comes from HonorHealth's own notice page and its insurance page, which carries an "Updated: July 15, 2026" date. I have not found a parallel statement from AZ Blue, so the account is HonorHealth's and I quote it as such.

HonorHealth says the carrier announced the network change on March 16, and describes it this way: "Effective July 1, 2026: AZ Blue Best Life (HMO) Medicare Advantage removed HonorHealth primary care providers (PCPs) from its network. This decision was made by AZ Blue and was outside of HonorHealth's control." The second step is the one that matters for this Annual Enrollment: "Effective Jan. 1, 2027: As a result of the change to the in-network status of HonorHealth's PCPs, as of Jan. 1, 2027, HonorHealth, including all employed physicians, specialists and facilities, will no longer be in network with AZ Blue Best Life (HMO) Medicare Advantage network."

Two sentences on the same page narrow it: "These changes apply only to AZ Blue Best Life (HMO) Medicare Advantage plans," and "HonorHealth continues to be in network with all other Blue Cross Blue Shield of Arizona plans." So an AZ Blue employer plan, a retiree plan, a Medicare Supplement sold by AZ Blue or an individual plan is not what this is about. It is the Medicare Advantage HMO family, and only that.

## This is a network change, not a plan ending, and that decides which doors are open

The reason this distinction matters is that a plan leaving Medicare opens doors that a plan changing its network does not. I wrote up [which Arizona plans are actually ending for 2027](/blog/medicare-advantage-plans-ending-arizona-2027/) last week, and the members of those plans got a non-renewal letter dated October 2, a Special Enrollment Period from December 8 through February 28, and a federal guaranteed-issue right to a Medicare Supplement with no health questions from November 1 through March 4. None of that attaches here.

What a Blue Best Life member got in September is an [Annual Notice of Change](/blog/medicare-annual-notice-of-change-letter-2027/), which says the plan continues and lists what moved. Medicare's own fact sheet on Advantage networks says a plan can add or remove providers at any time during the year and that a member whose provider leaves needs to choose a new one in the network. Medicare.gov lists a Special Enrollment Period for a significant change in a plan's provider network, evaluated case by case with two months to switch when it is granted, and HonorHealth's page says the one tied to the July change closed on July 31. Arizona has no Medigap birthday rule and no annual supplement window, so a Best Life member who would rather be on Original Medicare with a supplement goes through ordinary underwriting, unless they are inside a different protected window of their own, such as the first twelve months after joining an Advantage plan at 65. [What happens when a doctor leaves your network](/blog/what-if-my-doctor-leaves-my-medicare-advantage-network/) is the general version of this; this post is the specific one.

The windows that are open are the ordinary ones. Annual Enrollment runs October 15 through December 7, 2026, and a plan chosen then starts January 1, the same day HonorHealth comes out of the network. The Medicare Advantage open enrollment period, January 1 through March 31, 2027, allows one more move to another Advantage plan or back to Original Medicare for anyone who discovers in January that the new arrangement does not work. [When open enrollment starts and what it covers](/blog/when-does-medicare-open-enrollment-start-2027/) has the calendar.

Four cautions before anyone acts on that table, because it is the kind of list people screenshot and treat as settled.

**It is HonorHealth's list, not the plans' directories, and it is a 2026 list.** Networks reset in January, and HonorHealth itself says it updates this page in early January. The only document that decides whether your doctor is covered on January 1 is the plan's 2027 provider directory, checked by the doctor's name, not the hospital's logo. The same page that says HonorHealth is in a plan can be true for the building and false for the physician you see in it. [How Advantage networks are built](/learn/medicare-advantage-networks/) explains why the hospitalist, anesthesia and radiology groups inside a hospital contract separately from the hospital.

**Two plans on HonorHealth's list are not on the 2027 shelf.** HealthSpring Preferred (HMO), the plan HonorHealth lists with all of its locations participating, is not in the CMS 2027 file for any county; neither is eternalHealth Grand Give Back. A Best Life member who moves toward HealthSpring for HonorHealth access needs to be looking at the HealthSpring plans that continue, and those carry their own network notes. [The plans-ending post](/blog/medicare-advantage-plans-ending-arizona-2027/) has the detail.

**"Primary care" is the hinge.** For a Best Life member whose attachment to HonorHealth is a primary care doctor, most of the plans on that list do not solve the problem; by HonorHealth's own notes, only the UnitedHealthcare Phoenix Direct HMO Plans 1, 3 and 4 and the HealthSpring Preferred and Achieve plans carry HonorHealth PCPs, and one of those is ending. For a member whose attachment is a cardiologist, an orthopedic surgeon or the hospital itself, the list is much longer. Know which one you are before you shop.

**Check whether your doctor became an HonorHealth doctor this year.** HonorHealth completed the purchase of eleven Evernorth Care Group primary care clinics in January 2026, clinics it says were serving close to 80,000 patients. HonorHealth's July 1 change covered its employed primary care providers. If the sign on your clinic changed this year, ask the front desk whether your doctor is now an HonorHealth Medical Group physician, because the answer may put you inside this notice without your ever having chosen HonorHealth.

## If you would rather keep the plan

Staying is a legitimate choice, and for a Best Life member whose doctors are elsewhere it may be the right one. Here is what staying looks like, from HonorHealth's page and the CMS file. Classic stays at $0 with a $3,550 in-network cap; Plus goes to $49 with a $3,000 cap; the new Select plan is $0 with a $4,450 cap. HonorHealth says AZ Blue may assign a new in-network primary care doctor to members who stay, and that records can be transferred through CareLink or requested directly. From January 1, HonorHealth specialists, hospitals and outpatient facilities are out of the network, and on an HMO that generally means non-emergency care there is not covered. Emergency care is covered everywhere under every Advantage plan, so a trip to the Sonoran Crossing emergency department in an actual emergency is not the issue; a scheduled procedure there is.

HonorHealth also says continuity-of-care protections may apply to a member already scheduled for non-elective surgery or being treated for a serious or complex illness, an inpatient stay, a terminal illness or post-operative care, and that AZ Blue decides eligibility. If that is you, ask the plan now, in writing, and keep the answer. Medicare requires a plan that changes its network to protect members from interruptions in medically necessary care, and that protection is usually something you request rather than something that arrives.

## What a north Peoria or North Phoenix household should do before December 7

1. **Decide which HonorHealth you mean.** Pull twelve months of statements and sort the HonorHealth names into primary care, specialists and hospital. The answer to this notice is different for each.
2. **Check your doctors in the 2027 directories, by name.** Not HonorHealth's list, not a carrier's brochure, the directory for the specific plan, for 2027. Do it for every doctor you would mind losing, and write down the date you checked.
3. **Run the full-year number, not the premium.** The new caps on Classic and Plus are higher than this year's, and the Part D deductible rose. A plan with a higher premium and a cap you will never reach can cost less than a $0 plan you use heavily, and the worst year is the one to plan for.
4. **Price your own ZIP.** The Peoria–Glendale–Sun City seam puts neighbors a mile apart on different line-ups, and 85085 and 85086 on the I-17 side are a different quote again. [Why Peoria quotes differ by ZIP](/blog/medicare-plans-by-zip-code-peoria-az/) covers it.
5. **Be honest about the supplement door.** No guaranteed-issue window opens for a network change. If Original Medicare with a Medicare Supplement is where you would rather be, and you are in reasonable health, an underwritten application is possible, and the structural cost is the Part B premium of {{partB.premium}} a month in {{year}} that you already pay, plus the supplement, plus a stand-alone drug plan. [Plan G against Plan N for Arizona](/blog/plan-g-vs-plan-n-arizona-which-should-i-choose/) is the read if that is the direction.

## The dates
