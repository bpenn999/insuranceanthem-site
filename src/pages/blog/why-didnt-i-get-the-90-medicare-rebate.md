**If you did not get the $90 Medicare rebate, the most likely reason is one of three: you are in a Medicare Advantage plan, you pay IRMAA (the income-related surcharge on Part B and Part D), or Medicaid helps pay your Part B premium.** Any one of those ruled you out. The payment went only to people in Original Medicare Part B who live in the United States and are in none of those three groups. CMS puts that at about 20.8 million people, and KFF counts about 64 million people on Medicare, so most people got nothing.

I have had the question from several north Peoria households this month, usually from one spouse wondering why the other spouse's bank account showed $90 and theirs did not. The answer is nearly always in the list above, and in the 85383 corridor (Vistancia, Trilogy, Blackstone) it is more often IRMAA than anything else.

## What the $90 actually was

It was a **one-time Part B premium rebate**, paid in October 2026 from the Medicare Improvement Fund, a fund Congress created in 2008. According to CMS's own FAQ, published October 3:

- Most eligible people received it by **direct deposit from the Social Security Administration on or around October 8, 2026**.
- People without direct deposit were to get a **paper check from the U.S. Treasury later in October**, mailed to the address Medicare has on file.
- CMS says most recipients would also get an email or letter about it in mid-October.
- **Nobody had to apply**, and there was no fee.

It does not change your Part B premium, which is {{partB.premium}} a month in {{year}} for most people. It does not repeat, and it is a separate thing from the "Part B giveback" some Medicare Advantage plans advertise. That giveback is a plan benefit and is explained in [Is the Medicare Part B giveback worth it?](/blog/is-the-medicare-part-b-giveback-worth-it-peoria-az/)

**Medicare Advantage.** This is the biggest group. The AP put Medicare Advantage enrollment at roughly 35 million people, more than half of everyone on Medicare, and CMS's FAQ excludes all of them. In Maricopa County, where the Advantage menu is one of the busiest in the country, that covers a large share of retirees. A $0-premium plan, a giveback plan and a PPO with a monthly premium were all treated the same: not eligible.

**IRMAA.** This is the one that surprises people who chose Original Medicare with a Medicare Supplement precisely because they wanted the traditional route. If you pay IRMAA, you were excluded even though you are in Original Medicare.

For {{year}}, IRMAA applies when your modified adjusted gross income on your {{irmaaTaxYear}} tax return was above **{{irmaa.singleStart}} filing single or {{irmaa.jointStart}} filing jointly**. The first bracket raises Part B to {{irmaa.t1.partB}} a month and adds {{irmaa.t1.partD}} a month to Part D. The full table and how the two-year look-back works are in the [IRMAA explainer](/learn/irmaa-explained/), and the [IRMAA estimator](/tools/irmaa-estimator/) will place you in a bracket in under a minute.

A one-off spike in {{irmaaTaxYear}}, such as a home sale, a Roth conversion or a large capital gain, is enough to put you over. If that spike followed a life-changing event like retirement, you may be able to get the surcharge itself reduced; [How do I appeal IRMAA after retirement?](/blog/how-do-i-appeal-irmaa-after-retirement/) walks through Form SSA-44. CMS's FAQ does not say whether an appeal would change your rebate eligibility after the fact, so do not count on it, and ask your tax advisor how an appeal fits the rest of your plans.

**Medicaid premium help.** If AHCCCS or a [Medicare Savings Program](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/costs/help/medicare-savings-programs) pays your Part B premium, you were excluded. CMS counts that as premium assistance from Medicaid.

## Why one spouse got it and the other did not

Eligibility was decided person by person. In a Peoria household that is where most of the confusion comes from:

- **Different plans.** One spouse has a Medicare Supplement and the other is in an Advantage plan. The Supplement spouse qualifies (if no IRMAA); the Advantage spouse does not. Plenty of couples are set up this way on purpose, as I explain in [Can a husband and wife have different Medicare plans?](/blog/can-husband-and-wife-have-different-medicare-plans/)
- **One is not on Medicare yet.** A younger spouse still on employer coverage gets nothing, naturally.
- **Married filing jointly over {{irmaa.jointStart}}.** Both spouses on Medicare pay IRMAA, so neither gets the $90, even though both are in Original Medicare.

## If you are not drawing Social Security yet

A lot of the retirees I work with in 85383 deliberately delay Social Security and pay Part B on a quarterly Medicare bill instead. CMS says people without direct deposit were to be paid by a Treasury check mailed to the address Medicare has on file. If you moved to Peoria recently, especially from out of state, confirm that Medicare has your Arizona address. The same address question affects your bills; see [How do I pay my Medicare premium if I'm not on Social Security?](/blog/how-do-i-pay-my-medicare-premium-if-im-not-on-social-security/)

## How to check, and the calls to ignore

- **To ask whether you were eligible:** call 1-800-MEDICARE, as CMS's FAQ directs.
- **To ask where a payment is:** call Social Security; CMS says status questions go there from October 15, 2026.
- **Ignore anyone who contacts you about it.** There was nothing to apply for. A call, text or email offering to "release" or "process" your $90, or asking for your Medicare number or bank details, is not from Medicare.

## Do not switch plans to chase it

Open enrollment runs October 15 to December 7, and I have already been asked whether leaving an Advantage plan for Original Medicare now would "get the $90." It will not. The rebate was a one-time October 2026 payment, and any change you make now starts January 1, 2027. CMS's FAQ describes it as a single rebate.

There can be good reasons to move from an Advantage plan to Original Medicare and a Supplement, such as wanting no network or traveling often between Peoria and a second home. But in Arizona, outside a protected window, a Supplement carrier can ask health questions and decline you. Read [Can I switch from Medicare Advantage to Medigap in Arizona?](/blog/can-i-switch-from-medicare-advantage-to-medigap-in-arizona/) before you decide, and weigh the move on coverage, not on $90.

## The short version

- The **$90 was a one-time Part B rebate** paid in October 2026, mostly by direct deposit around October 8.
- You were **not eligible** if you are in **Medicare Advantage**, pay **IRMAA** ({{irmaaTaxYear}} income over {{irmaa.singleStart}} single or {{irmaa.jointStart}} joint), get **Medicaid help** with Part B, or live outside the U.S.
- Eligibility was **per person**, so spouses can differ.
- There is **nothing to apply for**. Treat anyone offering to process it as a scam.
- **Switching plans now will not get it for you.**
