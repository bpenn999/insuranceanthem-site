**The most popular Medicare Advantage plan in Arizona's largest market is AARP Medicare Advantage from UHC AZ-002P, an HMO-POS with 39,353 members in Maricopa County in September 2026, according to CMS's own enrollment counts. Humana Gold Plus H0028-074, HealthSpring Alliance and Blue Best Life Classic come next. All ten of the county's largest plans continue in 2027, but every one of them raises its drug deductible and seven raise the out-of-pocket cap.** Popular means large and familiar. It does not mean your doctors are in it.

Peoria sits inside Maricopa County, so the Medicare Advantage list a Vistancia or Westbrook Village household sees is the county list, give or take a ZIP code at the edge of a service area. The county is also where about half of Arizona's Medicare Advantage members live. When someone in Trilogy says "everybody here is on the same plan," this is the list they are describing.

## The ten largest Medicare Advantage plans in Maricopa County

CMS publishes a file every month that counts members by contract, plan and county. The September 2026 file is the latest one taken before this year's enrollment period opened, and it is the source of every member count below. The ranking covers general Medicare Advantage plans sold to individuals. It leaves out employer retiree plans, which you cannot join on your own, and special needs plans, which require a qualifying condition, Medicaid or a nursing-home stay.

Three numbers put the list in proportion. Maricopa had 57 general individual plans with members in September and 277,506 people enrolled in them. The top four held 133,053 of those people, close to half. The top ten held 201,310, about 72%. An industry analysis of earlier 2026 data by Mark Farrah Associates found the same four names at the top, so this is a settled order, not a one-month blip.

By company the race is closer than the plan list suggests. In general plans alone, Humana had 73,366 members and UnitedHealthcare 72,198, about 26% each. HealthSpring, the former Cigna business now owned by Health Care Service Corporation, had about 20%, AZ Blue about 11% and Aetna about 6%. Add the special needs plans and UnitedHealthcare pulls well ahead, with about 126,000 of the county's 371,000 individual Medicare Advantage members, because its dual-eligible plan is one of the largest in the county.

Read down the columns rather than across, and the pattern is plain. **No premium moved.** All ten were $0 a month in 2026 and are $0 in 2027. **Every drug deductible went up**, two of them from nothing. **Seven out-of-pocket caps went up**, by $450 to $1,200, and three held. The federal ceiling on drug deductibles rose from $615 to $700 for 2027, so some of this is the whole market shifting, but a plan that went from $0 to $200 made a choice, and the [drug deductible post](/blog/does-medicare-advantage-have-a-drug-deductible-2027/) has the county-wide picture.

The stars moved in both directions. Both Humana HMO contracts went up to 4.5. UnitedHealthcare's H0609 and AZ Blue's H0302 came down, and Banner's contract dropped to 3. A Star Rating is a score built from surveys, appeals, call-center tests and preventive-care measures across the whole contract; it is not a measure of how good your doctor is. The [2027 Star Ratings post](/blog/are-there-any-5-star-medicare-advantage-plans-in-arizona-2027/) explains what goes into it.

## The plan that folds into its own name

Row eight needs a careful reading. CMS's 2027 crosswalk file, released October 1, lists HealthSpring Preferred H0354-001 as a **consolidated renewal** into H0354-028, the plan sold in 2026 as HealthSpring Alliance and sold in 2027 under the Preferred name. In a consolidation the members are moved into the receiving plan automatically, and the letter they get is an Annual Notice of Change, not a notice that their plan is ending.

That has two practical effects for a Peoria member of H0354-001. First, doing nothing does not drop you to Original Medicare; it puts you in the renamed plan, at a $3,200 cap instead of $2,750 and a $500 drug deductible instead of $200. Second, a consolidation is treated as a renewal, so it generally does not bring the guaranteed-issue Medicare Supplement right that comes with a plan that is discontinued outright. Your own letter is the authority on which one you received. If it says your plan is ending, follow [the discontinued-plan steps](/blog/medicare-advantage-plan-discontinued-what-to-do-peoria-az-2027/); if it is an Annual Notice of Change, follow [the ANOC checklist](/blog/medicare-annual-notice-of-change-letter-2027/).

If both groups of members stay put, the renamed HealthSpring Preferred would hold about 42,300 people in January, more than any other general plan in the county. The two plans had different primary-care arrangements in 2026, so a member coming over from H0354-001 should look up their own doctor in the 2027 directory before assuming nothing changes.

## Popular is not the same as a fit

A plan gets large in Maricopa County for understandable reasons. It has been sold here a long time, it has a $0 premium, it is an HMO with a big local network, and a neighbor or a television advertisement recommended it. None of those tells you whether it suits you, and three things decide that.

**Your doctors.** Nine of the ten largest plans are HMOs or HMO-POS plans, and about 88% of general Medicare Advantage members in the county are in one. An HMO works well when your doctors are in it, and poorly when they are not. North Peoria makes this sharper than most of the Valley. A household in 85383 often has its primary care near Lake Pleasant Parkway and its specialists down at Arrowhead or over in Sun City, and the Peoria–Glendale–Sun City seam is exactly where network lists start to differ. Check every doctor by name in each plan's 2027 directory, and if the plan matters enough, call the office. The [Peoria service-area page](/service-area/peoria-az/) and the [Glendale page](/service-area/glendale-az/) have the local detail.

**Your prescriptions.** With every large plan raising its deductible, a drug list run through Plan Finder for 2027 is the only honest comparison. A plan that cost the least for your prescriptions in 2026 may not be in 2027.

**Your worst year, not your average one.** The out-of-pocket cap is the number for the year with a surgery or a hospital stay. The ten largest plans run from $2,995 to $6,100 for 2027, and the [out-of-pocket maximum post](/blog/medicare-advantage-out-of-pocket-maximum-peoria-az-2027/) shows where those sit against the rest of the county.

There is also a question that popularity cannot answer at all: whether Medicare Advantage is the right structure for you. Only about 12% of general Medicare Advantage members in the county chose a PPO. For a retiree who spends summers out of state, sees specialists outside the Valley, or simply wants no network and predictable costs, that route is worth pricing even if nobody on the street has it. [Medicare Advantage vs. Medigap](/learn/medicare-advantage-vs-medigap/) lays out both, and [switching from Advantage to Medigap in Arizona](/blog/can-i-switch-from-medicare-advantage-to-medigap-in-arizona/) explains the health questions that usually come with the move.

## Where the plans that actually ended sit

The plans that are ending in Maricopa for 2027 were mostly small. The four Aetna general plans that are not coming back held about 6,900 county members between them in September; Aetna Medicare Elite, the PPO that carried a 4.5-star rating, had 1,090. The two Wellcare plans and the eternalHealth plan that ended held about 1,300 more. Altogether that is roughly 3% of general Medicare Advantage members in the county. The [list of plans ending in Arizona](/blog/medicare-advantage-plans-ending-arizona-2027/) has every one.

So the 2027 story for most Peoria households is not a plan that disappeared. It is a familiar plan that kept its $0 premium and quietly moved its cap and its drug deductible. That is easy to miss if the Annual Notice of Change went into the pile with the flyers.

## What to do before December 7

1. **Find your plan's contract number** on your member card, such as H0609-027. Names change; the number is how you match your plan to the tables above and to your letter.
2. **Read the two numbers in your Annual Notice of Change** that this post tracks: the in-network out-of-pocket maximum and the Part D deductible.
3. **Run your drug list for 2027** in Medicare's Plan Finder against your current plan and two or three others available in your ZIP code.
4. **Look up each doctor by name** in each plan's 2027 directory, specialists included.
5. **Price the Medigap route once**, especially if you travel or want no network, so the comparison is real rather than assumed.
6. **Make any change by December 7** for a January 1 start. Enrollment opened October 15.
