  - q: Can husband and wife have different Medicare plans?
    a: Yes. Medicare is individual coverage. Each spouse has their own Medicare number, pays their own Part B premium and chooses their own coverage, so one can be on a Medicare Advantage plan while the other has Original Medicare with a Medicare Supplement and a Part D plan. Nothing requires a couple to match, and when doctors or prescriptions differ, matching is often the more expensive choice.
  - q: Do married couples have to enroll in Medicare at the same time?
    a: No. Each spouse enrolls on their own schedule, built around their own 65th birthday. The Initial Enrollment Period is the three months before the birthday month, the birthday month and the three months after. A spouse who is older can be on Medicare for years before the other is eligible, and enrolling one does nothing to the other's coverage.
  - q: Is it better for a husband and wife to have the same Medicare Supplement company?
    a: It can be cheaper. It is a pricing feature, not a coverage one. The plan letters mean the same thing at every company, so the question is whether the discount outweighs a better rate, or a better plan fit, elsewhere.
  - q: If my spouse's Medicare Advantage plan is discontinued, does that affect me?

What it does change is the household calendar, because your spouse now has a guaranteed right to a Medicare Supplement that ends about two months after their plan does, and the two of you may want to choose a carrier together before it closes.

**Yes. Medicare has no family or joint policy, so each spouse picks their own coverage, and a husband and wife can be on different Advantage plans, different Supplements, or one of each. In north Peoria this fall the question has a sharper edge: when one spouse's plan ends December 31 and the other turns 65 in early 2027, both of them can buy a Medicare Supplement without health questions, but each on a separate deadline, and the earlier one closes March 4.**

The call usually starts with "we." *We got a letter. We're turning 65. What should we do?* It's a fair way to think about it, since you share a kitchen table, a tax return and probably a pharmacy. Medicare doesn't work that way, though. It sees two people, two Medicare numbers, two premiums and two calendars. Once you see it that way, most of the confusion clears up, and in a year like this one the two calendars are the whole story.

## Medicare enrolls people, not households

Forty years of employer coverage teaches couples to think in terms of "employee plus spouse." Medicare drops that the day the first of you turns 65:

- **Separate enrollment.** Each spouse signs up during their own seven-month Initial Enrollment Period around their own 65th birthday. An older spouse can be on Medicare for years before the younger one is eligible. [Can my spouse get Medicare when I turn 65?](/blog/can-my-spouse-get-medicare-when-i-turn-65/) covers that gap.
- **Separate premiums.** Each of you pays the Part B premium, {{partB.premium}} a month in {{year}} before any surcharge, and each of you pays your own plan premium.
- **Separate plans.** One of you can be on an HMO, the other on a PPO, or one on Original Medicare with a supplement. A plan change for one spouse leaves the other's coverage alone.
- **Separate letters.** A non-renewal notice applies only to the person enrolled in the plan that is ending. If you weren't on that plan, the letter doesn't change your coverage.

The one place marriage still counts is premium-free Part A, which a spouse can earn on the other's work record. That gets settled once at enrollment and doesn't affect which plan either of you picks afterward.

## The 2027 collision: one plan ending, one birthday coming

Here is the household I am hearing from most this month. One spouse, already on Medicare, has a Medicare Advantage plan that the carrier is not renewing for 2027. A good number of Maricopa County plans ended this year, and [the full list is here](/blog/medicare-advantage-plans-ending-arizona-2027/). The other spouse turns 65 between January and April.

## Two Medigap windows with no health questions, open at once

Arizona has no birthday rule. Outside a protected window, a Medicare Supplement company can ask health questions and turn you down. This winter a couple may get two protected windows at the same time, and they work differently:

**The spouse whose plan is ending** has a guaranteed-issue right that runs from 60 days before the plan ends to 63 days after: November 1, 2026 through March 4, 2027. During it, any company selling in Arizona has to accept them for Plan A, B, D, G, K or L, with no health questions and no waiting period for pre-existing conditions. If they first became eligible for Medicare before 2020, C and F are on the list in place of D and G. Plan N isn't on the list. The application can go in from November 1 with a January 1 start, so there's no gap after the Advantage plan ends.

**The spouse turning 65** gets the six-month Medigap open enrollment period, which starts the month Part B starts. It's broader: any plan any company sells in Arizona, Plan N included, at the company's standard rate for their age, with no health questions. It also can't be repeated. Once those six months pass, the health questions come back for good.

So one spouse is on a short clock with a limited menu, and the other is on a longer clock with the full menu. The mistake I see is a couple treating them as one decision with one deadline. They then miss the March 4 date while they wait for the birthday, or they hurry the 65-year-old into a choice months before they need to make it.

The other route for the spouse with the ending plan is a different Advantage plan, chosen by December 7 for a January 1 start. They also have a special enrollment period from December 8 through February 28, but an enrollment in January starts February 1, with a month of Original Medicare in between. [What to do when your Advantage plan is discontinued](/blog/medicare-advantage-plan-discontinued-what-to-do-peoria-az-2027/) goes through those options one at a time.

## Splitting the household: when different plans make sense in north Peoria

Couples tend to match because it's easier to keep track of. In Vistancia, Trilogy and the 85383 corridor around Lake Pleasant Parkway and Happy Valley Road, these are the reasons I most often see a couple split:

- **Different doctors.** One spouse is in and out of a cardiology group at Arrowhead or a practice in Sun City. The other sees a primary care doctor once a year. A network that covers the first spouse well might be unnecessary for the second, or the other way around. On the Peoria–Glendale–Sun City seam, two plans with the same name can have different directories depending on the facility, so check each spouse's doctors by name. [Do I need a referral?](/blog/do-i-need-a-referral-medicare-advantage-peoria-az/) explains how HMO and PPO rules play out locally.
- **Different prescriptions.** Drug formularies are written plan by plan. A couple on one Part D or Advantage plan is often paying for one spouse's specialty drug to sit on a bad tier. Run each person's list separately on Medicare.gov's plan finder. Every enrollee's out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at {{partD.cap}} in {{year}}, and that cap is per person, not per household.
- **Different travel.** If one spouse spends summers in Flagstaff or with family out of state, and the other mostly stays home, a supplement's nationwide reach may matter for one and not the other.
- **Different rights.** This winter, the spouse with the ending plan can get a supplement without health questions now. The spouse turning 65 gets that chance once. A couple might reasonably decide that the person with a health history uses the protected window for a supplement, and the healthier spouse chooses an Advantage plan, knowing that switching back later would mean health questions.

None of these is a rule. They're the questions I ask before we decide whether matching makes sense.

## What a married couple shares anyway: IRMAA and the household discount

Two things stay joint even when the plans don't.

**The income surcharge.** IRMAA is set from the tax return filed two years earlier. For a couple filing jointly, it's tested against the joint brackets, which start at modified adjusted gross income above {{irmaa.jointStart}}. Each spouse on Medicare then pays their own surcharge on Part B and Part D. A plan change doesn't move it, in either direction. If the household's income has dropped since that return, the appeal is a separate conversation. [Does a Roth conversion affect Medicare premiums?](/blog/does-a-roth-conversion-affect-medicare-premiums/) covers the planning side, and your tax advisor should be part of it.

**The household discount.** Medicare.gov notes that Supplement companies may offer discounts to married people or for multiple policies. Some carriers apply it only when both spouses hold a policy with them. That's the one financial reason for a couple to use the same Supplement carrier, and it's why the order matters this winter. The spouse with the March 4 deadline should pick a carrier first. Then the spouse turning 65 can apply to the same carrier, if the discounted rate still compares well, whenever their own window opens. Discounts vary by company and change over time, so I quote them with and without.

## Order of operations for this winter

1. **Before October ends:** confirm which letter the spouse already on Medicare received, a change notice or a non-renewal, and write down the plan number. Write each spouse's doctor list and prescription list separately.
2. **Before December 7:** if the spouse with the ending plan is going to another Advantage plan, enroll for January 1. If they're going to Original Medicare with a supplement, choose the Part D plan in this window too.
3. **From November 1:** the supplement application for the spouse with the ending plan can go in, dated January 1. Get it done in November or December rather than waiting for March 4.
4. **During the Initial Enrollment Period:** the spouse turning 65 signs up for Parts A and B through Social Security, early enough that coverage starts in the birthday month.
5. **Once Part B has a start date:** the spouse turning 65 chooses a supplement, using the household discount if it helps, or an Advantage plan and a drug plan. Their six-month window gives them time to decide, but don't let it pass.
