**Right now, if a Medicare Advantage directory lists your doctor as in network and it is wrong, nothing in the rules automatically limits what you pay. That changes in 2028 under a proposed rule CMS published on October 20, 2026: if the directory listed the provider as in network on the day you booked, you would owe only the in-network amount.** For the plan you choose before December 7, the checking is still yours to do.

This is the first time Medicare has attached money to directory accuracy, so it is worth understanding. But the honest headline for a Peoria retiree shopping 2027 plans this fall is the second half: the protection arrives a year after the choice you are making now, and the directories you are reading today have the same weaknesses they had last year.

I see why it matters most in north Peoria. A household in 85383 — Vistancia, Trilogy, Blackstone — commonly has a primary care doctor near Lake Pleasant Parkway and Happy Valley Road, a cardiologist or orthopedist near Arrowhead, and a hospital preference that may sit across the Glendale line or down in Sun City. Every one of those is a separate listing that has to be right.

## What the proposed rule would change

The rule implements section 6220 of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026, which Congress titled the REAL Health Providers Act. It applies to network-based Medicare Advantage plans: the HMOs and PPOs that make up almost the whole Maricopa County menu.

Three details in the cost-sharing protection are better than I expected:

- **The test is the date you made the appointment**, not the date you were seen. If the listing was up at any point that day, it counts. Plans would have to keep archived copies of their directories for ten years so that can be checked.
- **Ties go to you.** If the plan cannot show when the appointment was booked, or cannot check its own archive in time, the proposal says the protection applies in your favor.
- **A "may be out of date" flag does not cancel it.** Even a listing marked as unverified would still qualify.

Any refund would come back to you, and the first Explanation of Benefits each year would have to explain the protection and give a phone number to ask for it.

## Why it does not help with your 2027 choice

Everything in that table starts with the 2028 plan year, and the accuracy scores do not appear until 2029. The plan you pick between now and December 7 runs from January 1 through December 31, 2027, under today's rules. There are 46 days left in this enrollment period.

It is also a proposal. Comments are open until **December 15, 2026**, and CMS can change the details before finalizing it. The core pieces are written into the law itself, so the 90-day checks and the cost-sharing protection are very likely to survive in some form, but the mechanics in the bullets above are CMS's proposed version.

And on a Medicare Advantage HMO, which is most of what Maricopa County offers, an out-of-network specialist visit is generally not covered at all outside an emergency or urgent care. That is a bigger exposure than a higher copay. According to CMS's 2027 plan landscape files, the general Medicare Advantage plans available in Maricopa County carry in-network out-of-pocket maximums from $2,000 to $8,900, and an out-of-network bill on an HMO counts toward none of it. For how HMO and PPO networks differ, see the [Medicare Advantage networks explainer](/learn/medicare-advantage-networks/).

## How often directories are wrong

The last nationwide check CMS published is old, but it is the best number there is. Between November 2017 and July 2018 CMS reviewed 5,602 providers at 10,504 listed locations across 52 Medicare Advantage organizations, and **48.74% of the locations had at least one error**: the doctor was not at that address, the phone number was wrong, or the listing said the doctor was taking new patients when they were not. Plan by plan, error rates ran from 4.63% to 93.02%.

Directories improve and slip as practices change hands. Around Peoria, practices are regularly absorbed into larger groups, and a group change can alter which plans a doctor takes without the doctor moving an inch. That is how a listing goes stale: nothing about the office looks different.

## How I check a Peoria doctor before December 7

Do this for every doctor you would be unhappy to lose, using the **exact 2027 plan name**, not just the insurance company:

1. **The plan's 2027 directory.** Make sure you are in the 2027 directory, not the 2026 one; plans run both this time of year. Take a dated screenshot of each listing. After 2028 that screenshot is your evidence; today it is still your best leverage.
2. **Medicare Plan Finder.** Search the same plan at [Medicare.gov's plan comparison tool](https://www.medicare.gov/plan-compare/) and add your doctors. Since 2026 plans have had to send their directory data there too, so it is a second reading of the same source, not an independent one.
3. **The doctor's billing office, not the front desk.** Ask: "Are you contracted with this plan name and number for 2027, at this location?" The location matters. A cardiology group can be in network at its Arrowhead office and not at a satellite clinic.
4. **The plan, if the three disagree.** Call the member or sales line, ask whether the provider is in network for 2027 at that address, and note the date, time and name of the representative.

The Peoria–Glendale–Sun City seam adds one more check. Plan line-ups change across a few ZIP codes, so confirm the plan is actually offered at your home address before you check a single doctor. The [Glendale service-area page](/service-area/glendale-az/) covers the other side of that line.

If you are moving off a plan that is ending in 2027, the same steps apply, in the order set out in the [discontinued-plan guide for Peoria](/blog/medicare-advantage-plan-discontinued-what-to-do-peoria-az-2027/). If your doctor actually leaves a network mid-year, that is a different problem; the [post on a doctor leaving your network](/blog/what-if-my-doctor-leaves-my-medicare-advantage-network/) covers it.

## If it happens to you in 2027

Suppose you book a dermatologist in January because the directory listed them, and the bill comes back as out of network. Until the 2028 protection exists:

- **Find the screenshot or print the listing** with the date it showed the provider as in network.
- **Ask the plan in writing to pay the claim at in-network cost**, citing its own directory. That is a request for an organization determination, and if the plan says no, you can appeal.
- **File a grievance** about the directory error itself, separately. Grievances are how CMS hears about patterns.
- **Call 1-800-MEDICARE** if the plan will not budge, and ask whether anything about your situation opens a way to change plans.

Plans do sometimes pay these when the member can show the listing, but under today's rules nothing obliges them to in the way the proposal would.

## Who should think about Original Medicare instead

If a directory error would be a serious problem for you, because you see several specialists, travel between Peoria and a second home, or want a particular Mayo Clinic Arizona or Banner physician regardless of contracts, the network question may matter more than the premium. Original Medicare with a Medicare Supplement has no network: any provider who takes Medicare takes you. The trade-off, and the health questions Arizona insurers can ask outside your protected windows, are laid out in [Medicare Advantage vs. Medigap](/learn/medicare-advantage-vs-medigap/).

## The short version

- CMS proposed on October 20, 2026 that, **starting in 2028**, you pay only in-network cost sharing when you relied on a wrong directory listing.
- Plans would also have to verify listings every 90 days, flag unverified ones and remove departed providers within 5 business days.
- **None of it applies to the 2027 plan you choose by December 7.** Check every doctor three ways and keep dated screenshots.
- Comments on the proposal close December 15, 2026.
