**Yes. If you are in a health sharing ministry, sign up for Medicare Part A and Part B during your own Initial Enrollment Period at 65.** A health share is not insurance and it is not a group health plan through current employment, so it does not give you a Special Enrollment Period later. Delay Part B and the premium rises 10% for each full 12 months you go without it, for life. A ministry is not creditable drug coverage either, so skipping a Part D plan adds a separate penalty of its own.

The rest of this post shows where those rules come from, what the senior programs of the ministries actually require, and how to move from a health share to Medicare without a month in between where neither one is paying.

## Why this keeps coming up in north Peoria

A lot of the households I talk with in [Peoria's](/service-area/peoria-az/) north end, in Vistancia, Blackstone and the 85383 corridor off Lake Pleasant Parkway, retired before 65 or work for themselves. Some bridged those years with a health sharing ministry because the monthly share amount was lower than the alternatives and the members shared their values. That is a reasonable choice at 58 or 62.

At 65 the rules change, and nothing in the ministry's paperwork tells you so. People assume their membership works the way an employer plan would, so they can wait on Medicare until they are ready. It doesn't work that way, and the reason is in how federal and Arizona law define a health share.

## A health share is not insurance, by Arizona's own statute

Arizona's law on these organizations is [A.R.S. §20-122](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/20/00122.htm). Its first line says a health care sharing ministry's practices "do not constitute the transaction of insurance business in this state." The definition that follows describes an organization that matches members who have medical bills with members who contribute, and that does **not** assume risk or promise to pay.

The statute also makes the ministry put a notice on its applications and guidelines saying, in substance, that it "is not an insurance company" and that its guidelines are "not an insurance policy," and that each member stays personally responsible for their own medical bills. If you have a membership packet in a drawer, that notice is in it.

That matters for Medicare because every protection that lets someone wait past 65 is tied to a particular kind of coverage, and a health share is none of them.

## Part B: no Special Enrollment Period for a health share

The rule that lets a 66-year-old still on a company plan sign up for Part B later without penalty is in [42 CFR §407.20](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/407.20). For someone 65 or older, the coverage has to be a group health plan "by reason of the current employment status" of you or your spouse.

A health share is not a group health plan, and membership is not tied to anyone's job. So:

- **Your window is the seven-month Initial Enrollment Period** around your 65th birthday. Someone turning 65 in February 2027 has a window from November 1, 2026 to May 31, 2027, and signing up by January 31 starts Part A and Part B on February 1. The [enrollment timeline tool](/tools/enrollment-timeline/) works out your own dates.
- **Miss it, and the only door is the General Enrollment Period**, January 1 to March 31 each year, with Part B starting the month after you sign up.
- **The penalty is 10% of the standard premium** (now {{partB.premium}} a month in {{figYear}}) for each full 12 months you could have had Part B and did not. It is added to every Part B bill for as long as you have Part B. The [late enrollment penalty calculator](/tools/late-enrollment-penalty-calculator/) shows what a given delay adds up to.

This is the same trap that catches people with an individual policy they bought themselves, which I covered from the business-owner side in [do I need Medicare at 65 if I'm self-employed](/blog/do-i-need-medicare-at-65-if-im-self-employed/). A health share is one step further from qualifying, because it is not insurance of any kind.

## Part D: a health share is not creditable drug coverage

The Part D penalty runs on a different test. Coverage protects you from it only if it is "creditable," and the federal regulation, [42 CFR §423.56](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/423.56), lists the kinds of coverage that can qualify. There are 13 of them: a Part D or Medicare Advantage drug plan, Medicaid, employer and union group plans, state pharmacy assistance, VA coverage, TRICARE, a Medigap policy with drug coverage, individual health insurance, Indian Health Service programs, PACE, cost plans, state high-risk pools, and anything else the Secretary designates.

A health care sharing ministry does not appear in that list. And a ministry will not send you the yearly creditable coverage notice an employer plan sends, because it has nothing to certify.

So if you rely on a health share for prescriptions after your enrollment window closes, the Part D penalty begins once you go 63 days in a row without creditable coverage. It equals 1% of the national base premium for each uncovered month, and it stays on your drug plan premium for as long as you have Part D. The [learn page on late penalties](/learn/late-enrollment-penalties/) walks through the arithmetic.

**One Arizona fact worth knowing before you shop:** according to CMS's 2027 plan landscape files, Arizona has 9 stand-alone Part D plans for 2027, down from 10, and **none** has a $0 premium. Four did in 2026. If you plan to pair Original Medicare with a separate drug plan, budget for a premium. The plan-by-plan table is in [how much are Medicare Part D plans in Arizona for 2027](/blog/medicare-part-d-plans-arizona-2027/).

## What the ministries' own 65+ programs say

Several of the larger ministries run programs for members 65 and older. The ones I have read are built to sit **behind** Medicare, not in place of it. At least one states plainly that it cannot process bill submissions unless the member is enrolled in both Part A and Part B, and describes itself as always secondary to Medicare.

So even if you plan to stay in a ministry, the ministry expects you on Medicare first. Read your own program's guidelines rather than assuming. Programs differ, and the guidelines are the only document that governs what a ministry shares.

## The decision that has a deadline: Medigap

For most of the north Peoria health share households I meet, the real question at 65 is what goes behind Original Medicare, not whether to enroll in it. The options are a Medicare Supplement plus a Part D plan, a Medicare Advantage plan, or a ministry's 65+ program.

The last row is the one people overlook. Your **Medigap open enrollment period** is the six months that start the month your Part B begins, and during it no company can turn you down or charge more for your health. It runs whether you use it or not. If you spend those six months in a ministry's senior program and decide at 67 that you want a Supplement, the application is underwritten. Arizona has no birthday rule and no annual window to reopen it. More on that window is in [when is Medicare Supplement open enrollment in Arizona](/blog/when-is-medicare-supplement-open-enrollment-arizona/).

A Supplement also travels. North Peoria households who drive to Arrowhead or Sun City for specialists, or who spend summers out of state, often find that matters more than the monthly price.

## How to make the switch without a gap

1. **Mark your Initial Enrollment Period now.** It opens three months before your birthday month. If your birthday falls on the 1st, Medicare counts you as turning 65 the month before.
2. **Enroll in Part A and Part B through Social Security** in the first three months of the window, so coverage begins on the 1st of your birthday month.
3. **Choose what goes behind Original Medicare** before Part B starts: a Supplement plus a Part D plan, or a Medicare Advantage plan. A Part D or Advantage choice made in your window takes effect when your Medicare does.
4. **Give the ministry notice only after your Medicare card and plan ID cards are in hand.** Ask for its cancellation terms in writing, and line the last month of sharing up with the first day of Medicare.
5. **Keep any bills from before your Medicare start date** going through the ministry's process, since Medicare will not pay for care received before coverage began.
