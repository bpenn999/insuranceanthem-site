**Sometimes. After December 7, the answer depends on which kind of Medicare you have.** If you are in a Medicare Advantage plan on January 1, federal rules give you one more change between January 1 and March 31. If you have Original Medicare with a Medicare Supplement and a stand-alone Part D plan, there is no second window. The drug plan you hold on December 8 is the one you keep through 2027 unless a Special Enrollment Period applies. The Annual Enrollment Period itself runs October 15 through December 7, and a choice made inside it starts January 1.

So December 7 is a hard stop for some households and a soft one for others. I spend this stretch of November with people in north Peoria, from Vistancia to the streets off Happy Valley Road, and the ones who get caught are almost always the ones who assumed the January window was theirs too.

## Advantage members: one more move, January through March

The Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period is written into 42 CFR §422.62. Anyone enrolled in an Advantage plan can make **one** election during the first three months of the year. You can move from one Advantage plan to another. Or you can leave Advantage entirely for Original Medicare and, at the same time, join a stand-alone drug plan.

Three limits matter.

- **One change, not one per month.** Use it in January and it is gone for the year.
- **It only runs outward from Advantage.** Someone on Original Medicare cannot use it to join an Advantage plan, and cannot use it to swap one stand-alone drug plan for another.
- **It is not a clean January 1 start.** Coverage changes on the first of the month after the plan receives your request. Your January-to-March choice still needs to be a deliberate one.

The reason I tell Peoria clients not to plan around it is networks. The case where the window really helps is someone who discovers in January that the cardiologist at Arrowhead or the group they use in Sun City is not in their new plan after all. That is a fixable mistake in February. A plan picked casually in December, on the assumption that you can redo it, often leaves you without a better option by March. The [Peoria service-area page](/service-area/peoria-az/) explains why plans that look identical on paper can reach different doctors in north and south Peoria.

## Supplement households: the window that does not reopen

This is the group the January window leaves out, and it is the group I worry about most this year.

If you have a Medicare Supplement, you are on Original Medicare, and your drug coverage is a separate Part D plan. Under 42 CFR §423.38 your routine chance to change that drug plan is the fall window, and only the fall window. Do nothing by December 7 and the plan you have rolls into 2027 at its 2027 price.

In Arizona the 2027 price is the problem. According to CMS's 2027 plan landscape files, there are nine stand-alone drug plans in the state for 2027 and none of them has a $0 premium. They run from $5.30 to $191.50 a month. The highest, AARP Medicare Rx Preferred, was $97.50 a month in 2026, so a household that keeps it by default pays nearly twice as much for twelve months. I went through every plan and why the prices moved in [how much are Part D plans in Arizona for 2027](/blog/medicare-part-d-plans-arizona-2027/).

The premium is only part of it. A cheaper plan that leaves one of your drugs off its formulary can cost more at the pharmacy counter. The comparison that matters is your actual prescription list, priced at the pharmacy you actually use, across all nine plans. Out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at {{partD.cap}} for {{year}}, rising for 2027, but the cap applies to drug costs, not the premium.

Higher-income households have one more reason to get this right: the Part D income surcharge is added on top of whatever plan you choose and goes to Medicare. A plan change does not move it up or down, so compare plans on premium plus expected drug costs and treat the surcharge as fixed.

## If your plan is ending

A non-renewal letter dated October 2 means your plan stops on December 31, and federal rules give you a Special Enrollment Period from December 8, 2026 through February 28, 2027. It is a safety net, not a second open enrollment. A plan picked in that period starts the first of the following month, so waiting past December 7 means at least one month on Original Medicare with no Advantage plan and possibly no drug coverage. The [step-by-step for a discontinued plan](/blog/medicare-advantage-plan-discontinued-what-to-do-peoria-az-2027/) has the full sequence, including the Medigap guaranteed-issue window that runs to March 4.

## The five-star exception barely reaches Peoria for 2027

There is one more year-round door, and it sounds bigger than it is. Federal rules let you join a Medicare Advantage plan rated **five stars overall** once during the year that rating applies. For 2027 that window runs from December 8, 2026 through November 30, 2027.

In Maricopa County it barely matters. In the 2027 Star Ratings CMS published on October 8, the only contract sold in Arizona with a five-star overall rating is H0710, a UnitedHealthcare nursing-home plan, an institutional Special Needs Plan for people who live in a qualifying facility. No general Medicare Advantage plan in the state is rated five stars. So for a north Peoria retiree living at home, the five-star exception does not open anything in 2027. I covered which Maricopa contracts landed at 4.5 and 4 in [are there any 5-star Medicare Advantage plans in Arizona for 2027?](/blog/are-there-any-5-star-medicare-advantage-plans-in-arizona-2027/) A rating is also a measure of the contract, not of your doctors. It is one column, never the whole decision.

## The supplement itself has no deadline, and no guarantee

December 7 does not apply to Medigap in either direction. You can apply for a different Medicare Supplement in March, July or any other month. What you cannot count on is approval. Outside your first six months on Part B and a short list of guaranteed-issue situations, the insurer can ask health questions and decline you, and **Arizona has no birthday rule** or annual window that waives them. Several websites claim otherwise; that rule exists in other states, not here.

So the realistic version is this. If you are healthy and want to move from one supplement to another, you can try any time of year. If you are in an Advantage plan and thinking about switching to a supplement in the January window, apply for the supplement **first** and drop the Advantage plan only after you are approved. [Can I change my Medicare Supplement plan anytime in Arizona?](/blog/can-i-change-my-medicare-supplement-plan-anytime-arizona/) covers the underwriting side in detail.

## Moving counts, but only a real move

A move out of your plan's service area opens its own Special Enrollment Period. Moving from Peoria to Prescott, for instance, takes you from Maricopa to Yavapai County and into a much shorter plan list. A move within the Valley usually leaves you in the same county and the same plans, so it does not count. Spending the winter here while your Advantage plan is based in another state is not a move either. If that is your situation, [do I have to change my Medicare plan if I move to Arizona?](/blog/do-i-have-to-change-my-medicare-if-i-move-to-arizona/) is the place to start. People who qualify for Extra Help also have more frequent chances to change drug plans during the year. The full list of special periods is on Medicare.gov's Special Enrollment Periods page, and the [enrollment periods explainer](/learn/medicare-enrollment-periods/) puts them all on one calendar.

## Four weeks left: what to settle before December 7

1. **Work out which row of the table is yours.** If it is the supplement-and-drug-plan row, December 7 is your only routine deadline for the year.
2. **Read your Part D plan's 2027 premium on the Annual Notice of Change.** If it went up sharply, price your prescriptions against the other eight Arizona plans now, not in January.
3. **In an Advantage plan, check the doctors first.** Look up each doctor and the hospital you would use in the 2027 provider directory, then call the offices to confirm, because directories are wrong more often than they should be.
4. **If you got a non-renewal letter, decide by December 7** so the new plan starts January 1 and the February 28 date stays a backstop.
5. **Thinking about a supplement?** Apply now, while you can still choose a matching drug plan before the window closes.
