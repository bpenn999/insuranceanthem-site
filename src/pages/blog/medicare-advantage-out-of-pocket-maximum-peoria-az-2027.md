**For 2027, no Medicare Advantage plan may charge you more than $9,850 a year for in-network Part A and Part B care, up from $9,250 in 2026. Plans sold in Peoria set their own limits well under that ceiling, from $2,000 to $8,900, and of the 38 Maricopa County drug plans that are renewing, 18 raised their limit for next year.** The number on your plan is the one that matters, and it is printed in your Annual Notice of Change.

The out-of-pocket maximum is the one figure that tells you what a bad year costs. Premiums and copays describe an ordinary year. The cap describes the year with a knee replacement at Arrowhead, a cancer diagnosis, or two hospital stays. For a north Peoria household in 85383, where the specialists and the hospital are usually a drive away from Vistancia or the Lake Pleasant Parkway corridor, that is the year worth planning for.

## The 2027 ceiling CMS set

CMS publishes three tiers each spring. A plan that picks a lower limit gets a little more freedom over individual copays; a plan that picks the highest tier gives up some of that freedom. The numbers below come from CMS's final 2027 Part C standards memo, dated April 22, 2026.

One detail in that memo is worth knowing before you read a plan's fine print. A plan at the top tier may not charge anything for the first 20 days in a skilled nursing facility, and may not charge more than Original Medicare for home health. A plan in the lower tier is allowed to charge up to $20 a day for those first 20 nursing-facility days. So a lower cap does not mean every line in the benefit summary is lower too. Read the skilled nursing row and the hospital row, not just the headline.

## What the cap counts, and what it does not

The out-of-pocket maximum is narrower than most people assume.

**Counts toward it:** deductibles, copays and coinsurance for covered Part A and Part B services in the plan's network. Hospital stays, surgery, specialist visits, imaging, outpatient procedures, ambulance rides, and Part B drugs given in a clinic, such as chemotherapy infusions.

**Does not count:**

- Your monthly plan premium and your Part B premium ({{partB.premium}} a month in {{year}}; the 2027 amount is announced in November).
- Any IRMAA surcharge. It is billed by Social Security, not by the plan.
- Prescriptions filled at a pharmacy. Those fall under Part D, which has its own separate cap of $2,400 in 2027. See [the Part D cap for 2027](/blog/medicare-part-d-out-of-pocket-cap-2027/).
- Dental, vision and hearing extras, and anything the plan does not cover at all.
- In an HMO, care from a provider outside the network, except emergencies and urgent care.

It is also per person. A married couple in Trilogy at Vistancia on the same plan has two separate caps, and each one starts over on January 1.

Of those 38, eighteen raised their in-network out-of-pocket maximum, nineteen kept it the same, and one lowered it. The average increase among the plans that went up was about $650.

## If your plan was merged into another one

There is one case where the change happens without you choosing it. CMS's 2027 files show the old HealthSpring Preferred (HMO), H0354-001, consolidated into HealthSpring Preferred, H0354-028. Members are moved automatically. The old plan's limit was $2,750; the plan they land in has a $3,200 limit for 2027. Same name, different plan number, a $450 higher worst case.

## How I use the number when comparing plans

The out-of-pocket maximum is the second thing I look at, after confirming the doctors are in network and before premiums or extras. Here is the order:

1. **Find your own 2026 and 2027 numbers** on the first pages of your [Annual Notice of Change](/blog/medicare-annual-notice-of-change-letter-2027/).
2. **Add up the realistic worst case** for each plan you are considering: the in-network out-of-pocket maximum, plus the $2,400 Part D cap if you take expensive drugs, plus twelve months of plan premium. That total is what a hard year costs.
3. **For a PPO, also note the combined limit.** If you plan to see doctors outside the network, such as a specialist in another state during summer travel, the combined number is the one that applies to that care. It is in the Summary of Benefits, not in the CMS landscape file.
4. **Check the hospital and skilled nursing rows.** A low cap with a high per-day hospital copay can reach the cap in a single stay. That is fine if you budgeted for it, and a surprise if you did not.
5. **Compare against Medigap.** If the worst-case total makes you uneasy, price a Medigap plan alongside. The trade-off is laid out in [Medicare Advantage vs. Medigap](/learn/medicare-advantage-vs-medigap/).

For higher-income households, step 5 is where the conversation usually goes. A Medicare Advantage cap of $5,000 or $6,000 is manageable money for many Peoria retirees, but it comes with prior authorization and a network. Medigap Plan G costs a monthly premium, and in exchange the main cost you carry for covered services is the {{partB.deductible}} Part B deductible, with any doctor in the country who takes Medicare. Arizona has no birthday rule for Medigap, so switching into it later usually means answering health questions, which is why the decision is easier to make while you are healthy.

## What to do before December 7

- **If your limit went up and nothing else changed for the better**, compare at least two other plans that include your doctors. The [Medicare cost estimator](/tools/medicare-cost-estimator/) helps put a whole year's cost on one line.
- **If your limit stayed the same**, still check the copays. A plan can hold its cap and raise the hospital or specialist copay underneath it.
- **If you are leaning toward Medigap**, start before December 7. You can drop a Medicare Advantage plan during open enrollment, but the Medigap application and any underwriting take time.

Open enrollment runs October 15 through December 7, 2026, for coverage starting January 1, 2027.
