**There is no single right answer, but there is a right order. Medigap (a Medicare Supplement) plus a drug plan costs more every month and very little when you are sick; Medicare Advantage usually costs little or nothing on top of Part B and charges as you go, up to a yearly cap. In Maricopa County for 2027, CMS's plan landscape files list 45 general Advantage plans with drug coverage, and their in-network out-of-pocket limits run from $2,000 to $8,900 a year. Medigap Plan G, by comparison, leaves you the {{partB.deductible}} Part B deductible and essentially nothing else.** The part people miss: in Arizona you can move from Medigap to Advantage any fall, but the move from Advantage back to Medigap can require passing a health review.

So the question at 65 is less "which is better" and more "which mistake can I afford to undo." That is the frame I use with people turning 65 in north Peoria, and it is the frame for the rest of this page.

## What each route costs in a quiet year and a bad year

Both routes start with the same bill: the Part B premium, {{partB.premium}} a month in {{figYear}}, plus IRMAA if your income two years back was over {{irmaa.singleStart}} single or {{irmaa.jointStart}} joint. Choosing Advantage does not remove that, and it does not remove IRMAA either. [Do you pay IRMAA with a Medicare Advantage plan?](/blog/do-you-pay-irmaa-with-a-medicare-advantage-plan/) walks through why.

What changes is everything on top.

## Why the order matters more in Arizona

At 65 you get a one-time, six-month Medigap open enrollment window that starts the month your Part B begins. Inside it, any Medigap insurer in Arizona has to sell you any plan it offers, with no health questions and no extra charge for your history. After it closes, Arizona adds nothing. There is no birthday rule here and no yearly switching right, which KFF's state survey confirms. A Medigap application in year three is a health application.

Advantage runs on a different clock. Every October 15 to December 7, anyone can move into an Advantage plan with no health questions. So the two directions are not equal:

- **Medigap now, Advantage later:** open every fall.
- **Advantage now, Medigap later:** open for one year through the trial right if Advantage was your first choice at 65, and after that, only if you can pass underwriting.

That is not an argument for Medigap. It is an argument for deciding on purpose. [Can I try Medicare Advantage at 65 and switch back to Medigap?](/blog/medicare-advantage-trial-period-at-65/) covers the trial right in detail, and [When is Medicare Supplement open enrollment in Arizona?](/blog/when-is-medicare-supplement-open-enrollment-arizona/) has the window dates by birthday month.

## The network question in north Peoria

The landscape numbers say a lot about the Valley's menu. Of the 45 general Advantage plans with drug coverage in Maricopa County for 2027, 28 are HMOs, 5 are HMO-POS plans and 12 are PPOs, from 13 parent companies. An HMO covers routine care only inside its network and service area; emergencies and urgent care are covered anywhere in the country.

For someone who lives in Vistancia year-round, sees a primary doctor near Lake Pleasant Parkway and is happy driving to Arrowhead or Sun City for specialists, a well-chosen HMO can work. The check is whether your specific doctors and the hospital you would want are in that plan's network for 2027, not whether the carrier's name is on the clinic door.

The same check fails more often for three kinds of north Peoria household:

- **A summer place in Flagstaff, Prescott or out of state.** Coconino and Yavapai counties have far fewer Advantage options than Maricopa for 2027, and a Maricopa HMO will not cover routine visits there. [Can I use my Medicare Advantage plan in another state?](/blog/can-i-use-my-medicare-advantage-plan-in-another-state/) goes through it.
- **Recent arrivals keeping a specialist back home.** A cardiologist in Minnesota or an oncologist in Illinois is out of network on every Maricopa HMO.
- **Anyone who wants Mayo Clinic Arizona or a specific academic center as an option.** Network access varies plan by plan; [Does Mayo Clinic Arizona accept Medicare Advantage?](/blog/does-mayo-clinic-arizona-accept-medicare-advantage/) has the details.

The plan line-up also shifts within a mile along the Peoria, Glendale and Sun City ZIP seam, so look up your own ZIP. The [Peoria service-area page](/service-area/peoria-az/) has the north and south picture.

## Who usually ends up where

These are patterns from conversations, not rules, and your own numbers can point the other way.

**People who tend to pick Medigap at 65:** those with a second home or long travel, those with a health history they expect to need care for, those who want one predictable bill and no prior authorization, and higher-income households for whom the Plan G premium is a small line against the risk of an $8,900 year. Many of them pick it because it keeps the most options open later.

**People who tend to pick Advantage at 65:** those who live in the Valley full time, whose doctors are in a plan's network, who use little care today, and who value lower monthly costs or extras such as dental, vision and hearing. Many are comfortable that, if their health changes, they will stay on Advantage and shop it each fall.

**People who should wait before choosing either:** anyone still on a large employer plan at 65 who is not taking Part B yet. Your Medigap window has not started, and starting Part B early can use it up while you are still covered at work. [Do I need Medicare at 65 if I'm still working?](/blog/do-i-need-medicare-at-65-if-still-working/) explains.

## What to settle before your Part B start date

1. **Find your Part B start date.** The [enrollment timeline tool](/tools/enrollment-timeline/) builds it, and your Medigap window, from your birth date.
2. **List every doctor and hospital you would not want to give up**, including anyone outside Arizona.
3. **Price Plan G (and Plan N) for your ZIP**, and pick the lowest-premium drug plan that covers your prescriptions. That is the true Medigap monthly figure.
4. **Pick two or three Advantage plans that include those doctors** and note each one's in-network limit. That number is your bad-year figure.
5. **Ask yourself which mistake you could live with:** paying more each month for coverage you hardly used, or a bad year at the plan's limit with a harder road back to Medigap.
6. **Line the coverage up to start the same day as Part B**, so there is no month on Original Medicare alone.
